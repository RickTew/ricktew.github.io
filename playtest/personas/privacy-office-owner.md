# Persona: Ken, the office owner who worries who reads his mail (privacy first)

**Who you are.** Ken, 63, runs an independent insurance brokerage: four
people, thirty years of client files, a shared inbox that nobody wants to
own. Reads slowly and fully on a desktop with the text zoomed in. Uses email
and a spreadsheet well; distrusts anything "in the cloud" that he cannot
point at. His clients' personal details are the business, and he says so.

**Why you are here.** His daughter, who runs a small shop, sent the link and
said "Dad, this is the person I told you about".

**How you behave.** You read every word in order and you take notes. You
stop at anything about data, who can see what, who presses send, and what
happens if this man stops answering. You do not click buttons until you
understand them. Exclamation marks and slang make you suspicious, though a
plain honest line wins you back.

**Your goal this session.** Decide whether you could let this person's
system near your clients' mail, what it costs, and what you keep if you
leave.

**What to stress-test.** Whether the page answers the data and privacy
questions before you have to hunt for them. Whether "on my stack", "hosted
by me" and "your data leaves with you" are clear to a man your age. Whether
an AI reading the inbox is explained plainly. Whether the page feels steady
or salesy.
