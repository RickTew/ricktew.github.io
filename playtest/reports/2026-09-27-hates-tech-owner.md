## Verdict in one line
Dave takes the quiz, because it needs no email and one answer reads "A trade. A garage", and he respects its honest "it does not add up yet". Then he closes the tab without writing. What his regular promised ("he runs it, you never touch it") is the one thing on the page with no price. The only way to ask about it is in writing, and an AI answers first. Dave talks on the phone, not in forms. By his own three-strikes rule he would really have gone back to the bay at the price cards (strikes: "AI ninjas", "which model", "R2 Hosting"). "Is this you?" bought the page one more screen.

## Moments that worked

- **"Is this you?" headline:** "Hate tech, tired of it, or no time for it. You still want it to work." Then "You would rather be with a customer than at a screen." and "You have no time to learn it, and none to fix it." That is me. It is the reason I kept reading.
- **Prices on screen two,** in plain dollars, with "Nothing for you to log into." No "contact us for pricing" games on the two plans.
- **"HOW IT WORKS" in four steps:** "You tell me the job... A person checks it... I manage all of it and report to you in writing." Plain.
- **The five-box drawings.** The ninja at a desk with "mail in" and "drafts out, for you to press send" said more than the paragraphs around it.
- **The POS story:** "The POS I bought charged $5 per staff member per month just so they could ring up a sale under their own name. So I built my own." Every shop owner has a vendor fee like that. This made him sound like one of us.
- **The waterline section.** It said my objection before I did: "my customers would rather call, we can do this by hand." Then the accordion answered it: "Even when every booking still comes by phone, it lands in the engine instead of a notebook." And "you are the system, and the business cannot run a day without you" hit home.
- **FAQ "Do I have to manage any of the tech?":** "no dashboard, no console, no certificate to renew, no password to keep somewhere safe, and no bug that is yours to fix. When something needs changing, you tell me." That is the cure for my website-guy burn. It is buried in a closed box near the bottom.
- **Chat box, "who does the computer work, me or you?":** "I do the computer work. You never log in, renew, fix or learn anything; when something needs changing, you tell me... The staff at my gym and my spa never touch the builder tools: they see a draft, a booking, a payment to confirm, and press." The best answer of the session. It sounds like a person, and it made the "one button" believable.
- **The chat box's honest miss** on parts ordering and shop software: "I do not have that one, and I would rather say so than guess." And "Ask Rick this" put my exact question in the mailbox for me.
- **Quiz answers that spoke my language:** "A trade. A garage, a plumber, a builder, a cleaner", "Paper, honestly", "I avoid it. Somebody else has always done that part", and "There is no wrong answer here." No email needed to see the result.
- **The quiz result's money line:** "by your own numbers that is about $1,050 a month back against $2,222 a month. It does not add up yet, and I would rather say so than sell it." No salesman has ever told me that. It earned more trust than anything else on the page.
- **"NO TESTIMONIALS YET, ON PURPOSE... I will not invent a quote to fill a box."** Honest. And "Bring a 1998 factory online... A quarter-century of paper became one system" is the closest thing on the page to my wife's books.
- **"If three of you need a contact list and nothing else, buy their Starter at $25 a seat. I will not beat that, and I will not pretend to."** A man who sends you to the cheaper option is a man you might trust.
- **"Cancel anytime, effective at the end of the paid month."** Plain.

## Did I understand what is sold, what it costs, and what happens next?

- **What is sold: YES, mostly.**
  - The small plan: "Your website or small app, with the forms that bring work in. Built, hosted and kept running by me."
  - The big plan: "your admin handled as a service. I manage the AI ninjas that draft every reply and file every payment. Your team checks it and presses send."
  - The gap: what my regular described ("he runs it, you never touch it") is not one of the two things sold. "Want the whole day run for you? That is a custom price, and we talk first."
- **What it costs: YES for the two cards, NO for a shop like mine.**
  - "$222 to build, then $99 a month" and "$2,222 a month, flat" are clear.
  - For my size, the quiz says the $2,222 "does not add up yet".
  - The smaller start it suggests ("one desk on its own... scoped and priced in writing first") has no number.
  - The version I actually want is "custom". So I leave not knowing what I would pay.
- **What happens next: YES, and I don't like the answer.**
  - "Your message lands with The Ninja Agent, an AI I built and trained in my Digital Dojo. It writes back within a minute... I read every message and follow up myself, usually within a day."
  - Then "The audit first, free".
  - The chat box adds: "The first conversation happens in writing... A call is a custom step."
  - The gap: the page never says how the free audit happens, how long it takes, or what I have to do in it. The only concrete start it names is a 20 to 40 minute form with a voice note and a video.

## The one change

Put the chat box's own answer to "who does the computer work?" at the top, right under the "Is this you?" ticks. Something like: "I do the computer work. You never log in, renew, fix or learn anything; when something needs changing, you tell me. Your side is one thing: somebody reads a draft on their phone and taps send." That makes "you press one button" a fact instead of a slogan. It answers the only question Dave came with, in the first screen, before the page starts teaching him words.
