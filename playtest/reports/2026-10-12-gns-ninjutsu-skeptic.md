# GNS round, 12 Oct 2026: Ken, the ninjutsu-curious history teacher

Start: https://ricktew.com/hininja/, on a laptop, from Google.

## Verdict in one line

A real teacher with real tapes, but the proof sits in a sealed file on the other page, and the question I came with ("where did his ninjutsu come from?") is only answered on About. I would write first, not buy.

## Legit or gimmick

First 10 seconds on HI: two relaxed weeks on Koh Samui, about 2 hours of training a day; it wants me to Enrol. Real training sold as a holiday, and "Not crazy serious like the camps of the old days" is someone under-promising.

Almost a gimmick: "Ninjitsu (the body)" (Hayes taught me ninjutsu; "ninjitsu" is the strip-mall spelling), "Rick Tew, the American Ninja" (a Dudikoff movie), and "25 years of real ninja training" with no teacher or lineage named anywhere on HI. Fakes leave exactly that gap. "In the press: Black Belt" kept me reading.

When I decided: GNS, inside the file, World file 01 United States: old photos (young Rick with a sword, a wall run, kicks in the woods) and "camps in the mountains of California by Yosemite". Then the TRMS 2000 tape: at 1:17 stick sparring on a lawn, at 4:02 students in belts drilling falls. Grainy and real. About then said it plainly: "Modern American Ninjitsu crossed with MMA", "Rick Tew's own systems." No fake lineage. I respect that; it belongs on the sales pages.

Votes: **HI: UNSURE** (claims, two photos, no footage). **GNS: LEGIT** (because of the file).

## The files

Yes, on my own. "Old tapes, real training, a very long record" is written for people like me, and HI sells the Gooffy link as "the vault of old tapes". What changed my view: the US photos and students on the TRMS tape. Europe helped a little.

If ONE thing should be visible without pressing: a silent 15 second loop from the TRMS 2000 tape, students rolling and stick drilling, in "Who teaches it" on HI and right under "What is it" on GNS.

## Who it is for, and not for

I picture backpackers in their 20s and 30s, a few beginners, one ex-fighter. The Muay Thai banner behind Rick's photo and "crossed with MMA" made me worry about fight-gym types; "Beginners to seasoned fighters" did not settle it. Nothing says who it is NOT for, except families lodging together. "Not a fight camp" would matter to me (the history page says it, buried).

## How I would have found it

Google: "ninjutsu training Thailand", "martial arts holiday for beginners". Also r/ninjutsu, r/martialarts, a DnD Discord. The sales pages never say "ninjutsu"; only the quiz does. An ad that stops me: "No fake lineage. Thirty years of teaching. Here are the 2000 camp tapes. Two relaxed weeks on a Thai island."

## What I would tell a friend

A holiday with training, not a hard camp. 8 to 10am Mon to Fri, optional evening review. Rick teaches at least 2 classes a week, a Guide the rest. His own modern system plus WinJitsu (mind). Hostel bunks, simple meals, a game room, a mall gym on Koh Samui. $2,222 for two weeks, $1,000 per extra week, an app month and a T-shirt. Flights apparently extra.

## Buy, write or leave

Write first, from the HI form: a July date? Who is the Guide? Typical age? Where did Rick learn? To buy: a summer date, a named Guide, one plain line on where the art comes from, and recent student footage.

## The one change

Near the top of both pages, one honest line and one clip: "My own system, Modern American Ninjitsu, no claimed Japanese lineage. Here is training in 2000," beside the TRMS 2000 loop.
