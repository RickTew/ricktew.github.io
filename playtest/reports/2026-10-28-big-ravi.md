# Ravi, 34, ML engineer on Samui for three months (phone first, then laptop)

## Verdict in one line

A real teacher with a real history and a belt path my RPG brain loves, but at $3,997 for two months of group classes I need to know who is in the room, how many of us, and where it is before I pay.

## First 10 seconds

"Martial Arts Ninja Course by Rick Tew", a photo of Rick demonstrating on a student, "on Koh Samui". A ninja course for visitors; it wants me to Book Now or pick tactics. The yellow friend-free banner caught my eye at once. I never learned what "HI" in "HI NINJA" means. I would press the tactic tiles first, not Book Now.

## Would I book

Ask first, not book. The stay I would pick is **2 months, $3,997, the yellow belt**: I am here three months, a belt is a clear level to hit, and per week it is far better than 2 weeks at $2,222. What stops me:

1. **Start date.** "Start any day from 15 January 2027." I am on Samui from now through January, so only my last two weeks overlap. That alone kills the belt for this trip.
2. **Who teaches.** "Rick builds" the seminar, and "Rick often rotates between the morning and evening sessions when he is available." So some days it is not Rick? Then who? At about $500 a week that is the whole question.
3. **Group size.** "Private group workshop" with no number. Five of us or twenty-five?
4. **Where.** "Koh Samui" is the only address. My desk is in Lamai. I only learned it is near Big C and Starbucks by clicking through to NerdHostel.
5. **Price against Samui.** Gyms here sell nomads a month for a few thousand baht. I accept paying more for Rick's system, but nothing says I get more Rick.

To book I need: a start before January, a named teacher for the mornings, a group size and a map pin.

## The photos

Mostly yes. What convinced me: the hero shot (students lined up, real mats), the NinjaGym photo with the climbing wall (the only one that looked recent), the bunk room, and the timeline video ("At 15, already on TV").

Off: most class photos look 10 to 15 years old and are mostly fit white guys in black tees. A couple of brown faces, but nobody like a stiff desk worker. The rotating boxes annoyed me: twice I caught a ghostly mid-fade double exposure, and the Rick box opened on a banner cropped to "ICK TEW". On the laptop, six boxes side by side worked much better than the long phone column.

## The bonuses

WinJitsu: Rick's "mental martial art" for focus and goals: coaching, a month of the app, and books that only join "from orange on", so not for my yellow belt. A bonus, but a self-help one; neutral for me.

Pocket Dojo: an app with every lesson for all five belts, footprint diagrams, "mark each lesson practiced and watch your count grow", and a box to ask Sensei Tew. This sold me: I want a skill I keep practising after I leave, and a progress counter is my thing. It made $3,997 feel more worth it. But it never says how long I keep access, and the box's biggest button, red "SEE THE HOME STUDY PROGRAM", reads like a second thing to buy.

## Welcome

Yes for a careful beginner: "Built for beginners: every drill has an easier version", "Not a boot camp", "Not a fight camp. No sparring unless you ask", and the FAQ "Got an injury, or not very fit? Tell me before you come." The "Travelers and nomads" and "Gamers and nerds" boxes spoke straight to me. Less welcoming for my back: rolls and falls are two of the eight areas, and the hero of the Rick section is a full split. A line on mobility or desk-job backs would have helped.

## The chat

I asked: "I work a remote job on weekdays and already have my own room in Lamai. Can I do only the evening class and still earn the yellow belt?" It answered "How do the belts work?" with the belt price. Not my question. I rephrased: "Can I train only the evening class?" It gave the daily schedule, again no yes or no, even though the page itself says "Not a morning person? Come to this one instead." I asked "Where exactly is NinjaGym on Samui?" and got "Koh Samui, Thailand." Fast, but no help.

## Legit or gimmick

The person is real: childhood TV clips, the magazine list, NinjaGym, photos across years. I decided that at the timeline video. Worth the money is the open part, and the selling got heavier lower down: "Only 12 spots left for 2027", "save $2,000", friend free. The ladder confused me: one belt is $3,997, yet Instructor is all five belts in a year for $8,994, and Mastery the same five belts slower for $15,988. And a self-named "Modern American Ninjitsu" is what Reddit flags, so I check reviews first.

**Vote: UNSURE**

## Moments of friction

1. When section / expected a start soon / "from 15 January 2027" / deflated, I am here now / blocker
2. Schedule note / expected "Rick teaches you" / "when he is available" / at this price, worried / would-quit
3. Price cards / expected group size / none anywhere / cannot judge value / would-quit
4. Where / expected an area or a map / "Koh Samui" only, chat the same / had to leave the page / annoyance
5. Chat / expected yes or no on evenings only / generic belt and schedule cards / felt unheard / annoyance
6. Price ladder / expected per-belt price to drop with length / Instructor cheaper per belt than the belt program, Mastery dearer than Instructor / distrust / annoyance
7. Mastery card / expected calm info / spots-left badge plus discount plus promo / felt like a funnel / annoyance
8. Rotating photos / expected a clean picture / caught mid-fade, ghost blend; banner cropped "ICK TEW" / looks broken / cosmetic
9. Length / about 36 phone screens, Mastery and Instructor pitches in the middle of an intro page / skimmed / annoyance

## Moments that worked

1. The Ninja Menu: ten stances tagged by belt colour; I picked Horse Stance, Natural Reactions, Front Kicks, Fist Strikes and Duck and got "Your Ninja Week is ready". A skill tree. Keep it.
2. The belt grid, months 1 to 10 in colours, and the yellow belt list. Clear levels.
3. "Class, then the day is yours" and 8 to 10am fits a remote job well.
4. Pocket Dojo's practice counter.
5. "Already have a place? The price is the same: you pay for the training." Honest, even if I do not love it.
6. The friend-free promo: I would forward it to my coworking chat.
7. "Answers Rick wrote. No guessing." is an honest chat header.

## Words I did not understand

HI (in HI Ninja), "private group workshop", Sensei Seminar, PSC, RTMS (until the System section), "tactics" used for techniques, Guide with a capital G, Combat Reality, "Mini-Martial Art System via Stances", Tew's Tour.

## Buy, write or leave

Write. I would use the question box: can I start in November or December, who teaches the mornings, how many per class, can evenings count toward the belt. Then Reddit and Google Maps reviews.

## The one change

Beside the price cards, say plainly how many people are in a class and how many of my sessions Rick himself teaches. That is the gap between "pricey Samui gym" and "worth it".
