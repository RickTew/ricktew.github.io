# Editor's map: what /aininja/ does not need, 2026-10-01

Report only. Nothing on the page was changed. Read from `aininja/index.html` at
`2ad3d47` (1 Oct 2026), the same file Pages serves. Word counts come from a
script that strips tags, scripts, styles and SVG, and counts `<details>`
bodies separately because they only show after a tap. Two kinds of text are
not in the counts: the five-word boxes in #you (about 95 words, written in by
script, one box at a time) and the quiz result (built from the answers).

Who it is read for: an owner of a 2 to 15 person business (a garage, a
clinic, a cleaning company, a studio) who hates tech, is tired of it, or has
no time for it, and wants the job done.

## In one paragraph

The page is **9,134 words in 29 blocks** (24 sections with an id, the door
bar, the nav, the summit card, the two halves of the story, the footer):
7,001 show without a tap and 2,133 sit behind a tap, mostly the FAQ answers
(1,098) and the solution rows (683). This client needs roughly a third of
it: #you, a slice of the story, the solution summaries, the #dojo cards, the
quiz, nine FAQ answers and the mailbox, about 2,500 words of reading plus
the quiz. The offer is clear early: all four answers (what, who does the
computer work, cost, next step) land inside #you by about word 420 of the
page. The single biggest source of redundancy is **the offer being told
again and again**: the two plans and their terms are written out in #you,
in the #dojo cards, in the 171 word paragraph under the #dojo cards, inside
#claudeforce (a word for word copy of the #you cards) and in the FAQ cost
answer, and "a person presses every send" is said 29 times. Behind that sits
a structural cause: alternate versions of the same section were kept side by
side (three openings: #top, #opt-2c, #opt-2b; three "about" blocks: #opt-6a,
#ninja, #opt-6b; three "how it starts" lists: #dojo steps, #opt-5a, FAQ
first month). And the page offers about nine things, only two with a price,
which is where "confused about what we offer" comes from now, not the top.

## The moment of clarity

**Where it lands: #you, at about word 210 of the section (about word 340 of
the page), complete by word 286 (about 420 of the page).**

- What is sold: "Your website or small app, with the forms that bring work
  in. Built, hosted and kept running by me." and "All of that, plus your
  admin handled as a service."
- Who does the computer work: "You press one button and I think about the
  rest: the tech, the AI, which model is best this month, the thing that
  broke at 2am. None of it is yours to carry." (word 107) and "I manage the
  AI ninjas that draft every reply and file every payment. Your team checks
  it and presses send."
- What it costs: "$222 to build, then $99 a month" and "$2,222 a month,
  flat".
- What to do next: the "I want this Tew" button under each card, How it
  works step 1 "You tell me the job." and the "Take the quiz" button.

The early sections do their job. The September fix (who does the computer
work) is in place.

**What is missing before then, or right after:**

- **The free audit.** The real first step is not named in #top or #you. The
  word "free" first appears on the #dojo card, about word 4,400. A
  hates-tech owner pressing "I want this Tew" does not know the next thing
  that happens is a free audit, in writing.
- **What the button does.** "I want this Tew" does not say it drops a note
  into a form at the bottom of the page; only the nav line ("Push the button
  and go") hints at it.
- **The two product names.** "Your Dojo" is on a card before the five boxes
  explain "Dojo" (one block lower). "R2" is explained only on the #dojo card
  ("R2 is me, Rick Tew"), about 4,300 words after the client first sees it.
- **No calls.** "report to you in writing" (step 4) is the only hint; the
  page never says plainly that it is all in writing and there are no calls.
  Dave (27 Sep) left partly because he could not phone.
- **The long gap.** After #you, the client scrolls about 3,800 words (the
  summit card, the story, #opt-e1, #outcome, #operating, #opt-2b,
  #solutions, #masters) before the plans come back with their details in
  #dojo.

**The early section that carries the most confusion: the summit card**
(`div.noise`, #summit, 378 words, right after #opt-2c). It changes the
subject from the client's business to social media millionaires and a
YouTube summit, it is labelled "This week" over a 14 September date, and it
asks for reading two lines after #opt-2c says "Reading is not required."
Runner-up: #opt-2c itself, which repeats the hero's kicker so the page
seems to start over. Inside #you, the five boxes add five new words in a row
right after the clearest lines on the page.
