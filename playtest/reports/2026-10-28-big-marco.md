# Marco, 42, Toronto: returning karate black belt with a bad knee

## Verdict in one line
The teacher and the syllabus look real, but nothing tells me what my knee will actually do on the mat, and $1,111 a week for two hours a day in a bunk bed is triple the Muay Thai camps I priced, so I would write first, not book.

## First 10 seconds
Phone: "Martial Arts / Ninja Course / by Rick Tew" over three adults in a fighting stance, one about my age. "Ninja Course" made me wince. Then Book Now twice and a "friend for free" bar before any price. Laptop adds eight gold icon tiles asking "What do you want to learn?". A martial arts holiday on Koh Samui for beginners and travellers; it wants me to book. I press nothing yet and scroll for the price and the teacher.

## Would I book
Ask first. I would pick 1 week at $1,111 to test my knee (I only found it in the small print under the price cards), then maybe extend. What stops me:
1. My knee. "Tell me before you come. Every drill has an easier version." is all I get, and the yellow belt has rolls, flat falls, a jump and a low sweep.
2. The money. Muay Thai camps I looked at are $300 to $600 a week with a room and two sessions a day. Here it is $2,222 for two weeks, 8 to 10 am Monday to Friday plus an optional evening class, and a shared bunk dorm.
3. Who is on the mat. The headline says "by Rick Tew", the schedule says "Rick often rotates ... when he is available". I am paying for Rick.
To book: a straight knee answer, a recent adult class clip, and Rick on my mornings.

## The photos
They make it real; not stock. Convinced me: the laptop hero of a teacher in a blue gi and black belt correcting a student's arm while a dozen adults watch; the 8-photo class grid; the older man in a neon shirt with a training knife under "Every age, every level" (finally someone near my age); the quote cards with faces. Looked off: many are old point-and-shoot shots in the same wooden hall; a guy tumbling over a plastic chair in the Rolls box (not with my knee); the teen in a ninja suit on a roof in the Rick video, exactly the fake ninja stuff I roll my eyes at; bunk beds on a kids' foam puzzle mat. Mostly twenty-somethings. Rotation: more pictures helped, but on the phone I caught two photos ghosted mid-fade, and six boxes changing at once on the laptop is busy.

## The bonuses
WinJitsu: Rick's mindset system; coaching on focus and goals, five books (only from orange belt, so not for me), a free month of an app. Pocket Dojo: his home-study curriculum on a phone, belt by belt, with foot diagrams and questions to him. Both say "with every stay", so they read as bonuses. But the "Every stay" list by the prices names WinJitsu and a T-shirt, not Pocket Dojo, so is it included, and for how long? The red "See the Home Study Program" button reads like an upsell. WinJitsu feels like self-help padding; Pocket Dojo is the useful one. Neither moved the price; I am buying mat time.

## Welcome
Yes: "Trained before? Welcome too." "Not a fight camp. No sparring unless you ask." "Not a boot camp. Every drill has an easier version." "Am I too old? No." "The old camps were hard core ... This intro is not that." The older man photo. No: it is pitched at 25-year-old backpackers ("Gamers and nerds", race car simulator, Nintendo on 75-inch screens), the bunk dorm, and a one-line injury answer. I am welcome, but not the guest it was written for.

## The chat
I asked: "I'm 42 with an old knee injury and a black belt in karate from years ago. Can my knee handle this, and how hard is the training?" It answered "How do the belts work?": it grabbed "black belt" and ignored the knee. Retried "Can I train with a bad knee?": the same FAQ line plus "Tell Kuno about it". "How many hours of training a day?" got a clear schedule. Useful for the schedule, useless for my real question.

## Legit or gimmick
The man is real: Black Belt magazine, teaching since his teens, Holland, NinjaGym. I decided the training is real when I opened Strikes in the menu: palm heel, jab cross hook uppercut, jam block and run, each with a belt colour. A syllabus, not a costume. What keeps me off LEGIT: belts every two months (red in ten months, as a karate guy that smells like a belt factory), "a martial science, not a style", "Only 12 spots left for 2027", and the price. Vote: **UNSURE**, leaning legit.

## Moments of friction
1. Chat / knee question / expected an injury answer / got the belt program / felt like a vending machine / annoyance.
2. FAQ "Got an injury" / expected what the easier versions are / got "Tell me before you come" / I cannot book on that / blocker.
3. Price cards / expected why it costs triple a Muay Thai camp / got four big numbers, $1,111 a week hidden underneath / sticker shock / would-quit for many, ask-first for me.
4. Belt ladder / expected the bigger plans to be better value / $3,997 a belt, but all five belts with an instructor certificate is $8,994 in a year, and Mastery is $15,988 for the same belts slower / I sell for a living and this does not add up / annoyance, hurts trust.
5. Promotions / three at once (friend free, save $2,000, 12 spots left) / pressure tactics / annoyance.
6. Headline "by Rick Tew" against "when he is available" / who teaches my morning? / ask-first.
7. Pocket Dojo "with every stay" but missing from "Every stay" by the prices / unsure what I get / annoyance.
8. Phone / the round chat button covers caption links ("Pick Ninja Tools"), photos caught mid-fade / cosmetic.
9. The Mastery band (move to Thailand for 2 years) sits mid-page on a two-week intro; long scroll to the price / annoyance.

## Moments that worked
- The "What it is not" box: no sparring unless you ask, not a boot camp.
- "Trained before? Welcome too."
- The Ninja Menu technique lists with belt colours and one-line descriptions. Picking 5 felt like building my own lesson plan, and the price came after.
- The plain schedule: 8 to 10 am, optional evening, weekends free.
- Student quotes with names and faces, especially "He knows exactly how far he can push his students."
- Prices shown openly; dates that can move within a year is honest.
- "The old days ... This intro is not that."

## Words I did not understand
HI Ninja (what is HI?), tactics (you mean techniques?), Sensei Seminar, PSC Self-Defense Program, RTMS, Mini-Martial Art System via Stances, Combat Reality, Guide (capital G), Pocket Dojo vs Home Study Program (same thing?), Strategic Intervention Coach, Robbins-Madanes, Modern American Ninjitsu.

## Buy, write or leave
Write, in the question box under "An injury, health or fitness". A specific answer and I book one week.

## The one change
Answer the bad-knee question for real: under "Got an injury" and in the chat, say what the easier version of rolls, falls, jumps and kicks actually is, and make the chat answer the injury part first even when the sentence also says "black belt".
