# Priya, the clinic owner with fifteen minutes - 2026-10-01 (round six)

Read on a phone: headless Chromium with the iPhone 13 profile (390 x 664, touch), live page at https://ricktew.com/aininja/. The page is 42.5 phone screens tall. About 20 minutes in character.

Safety: both mail endpoints were blocked by a route set before every page load, in every run (scroll, chat box, quiz, mailbox, quiz hand-off). Nothing ever tried to reach them: the block counter read 0 on every run, and the mailbox and hand-off runs saw no POST of any kind. "Drop it in my mailbox" was never pressed. The quiz result's "Send me the breakdown" email box was not touched. Name and email were typed only into the visible, labelled "Your name" and "Your email" fields, as "Priya Example" and priya@example.test.

## Verdict in one line

I would write, a short one. The chat box told me inside three minutes that bookings and reply drafting exist and cost $2,222 a month, but I would only write to ask the three things the page never answered: does it work with the booking software I already have, does it do WhatsApp, and is there anything under $2,222 for just those two jobs. In real life I would have stopped reading at screen 8.

## Moments of friction (the core of the report)

1. **Top screen, "I'm not a marketing expert. I'm the person behind your AI ninjas."** I expected bookings or replies to be named somewhere. What I got in about 10 seconds was the gist: he builds something with AI for a business, and "either you run it yourself or I run it with you". Nothing on the screen said bookings or replies, so I went straight to Ask, which is what I do anyway. Feel: fine. Severity: cosmetic. (This is the top screen, so I am only reporting the time it took.)

2. **Chat box, "can it write replies to patient messages on whatsapp".** It answered "Can I use it to answer my emails?" and put the WhatsApp answer behind a second tap ("Also close: Can it answer WhatsApp, LINE or Facebook messages, not just email?"). That answer reads: "Email first ... Another channel is a build, not a switch." My patients message on WhatsApp, so the thing I need most is an extra build with no price on it. Severity: annoyance.

3. **Chat box, "we use cliniko, does it work with that?"** It answered a different question: "Do I need to understand AI to use this?" I tried again with "does it connect to the booking software we already use" and got "We already use AI. Why would we need this?" Neither time did it say it did not know, even though its own welcome says "if I do not have one, I hand you to him". For a clinic, this is the question that decides it. Feel: it dodged me twice. Severity: annoyance, close to would-quit.

4. **Chat box, "can i get just the booking part for less money".** It answered "What is The Money Desk?", apparently because I said "money". The real answer turned out to be in the FAQ ("Can I get just one desk?": same $2,222, or a custom quote with no number). Severity: annoyance. That makes 3 of my 10 chat questions that got another question's answer.

5. **Chat box, "how much is it".** The answer is three paragraphs, about 250 words, to give me one number. The price card on screen 5 does it in about 15 words. Severity: annoyance.

6. **"Is this you?" band, the two price cards (screens 4 and 5).** The $222 card says "a website of a handful of pages with a contact or booking form". The $2,222 card says "draft every reply and file every payment" and never says bookings. Bookings only show up a screen later, in How it works step 2. So which plan covers my bookings, the $99 one or the $2,222 one? I had to work it out from the chat box. Severity: annoyance.

7. **"The first questions", straight under How it works (screens 6 and 7).** The "What does it cost?" answer repeats both cards in full, word for word what the chat box gave me. Severity: annoyance.

8. **"Five words · five boxes" (screen 7).** A glossary for Dojo, ninja, seat, belt and sensei. If a page needs a dictionary, the words are the problem. I skipped it. Severity: cosmetic.

9. **The story, "Five months. Solutions and goals. One reason." (screens 8 to 22).** Fourteen phone screens about a kids' gym, songs, a till, books from his twenties, a childhood board game, the App Store, a console called TewBeDo and a habit app. Nothing here is about my clinic, except two lines: the gym's "4,996+ app bookings since April", and the friend whose "support desk drafts a reply to every customer mail". **This is where I would have stopped reading in real life.** Severity: would-quit (for reading, not for the offer).

10. **"The website is not the machine. It is the handle." (screen 24).** The headline told me nothing. Handle, waterline, engine, "ten percent above the water": I had to work to decode it, and for a moment I wondered if it was a third thing to buy. The list under it ("Every booking, recorded ... Agents drafting the email") was the useful part, and I nearly flicked past it. Severity: annoyance.

11. **"Somebody told you to get Claudeforce? Here is the honest bill." (screens 34 to 36).** Nobody told me. I have never heard of it. I jump to prices, so I landed on "$38,400 to $66,000", "+ $125" and "about 10 cents" in the biggest type on the screen, and for a second I thought those were his prices. Severity: annoyance.

12. **Quiz question 9, "Anything you have wanted built for years and never built? Mine was a game with my name on it."** Off topic for someone who just wants the inbox quiet. Severity: cosmetic.

13. **Quiz result.** "Some of what you described is not a desk sitting on a system you already own, it is the system." I told it I rent software. Does that mean he replaces my booking system? I did not understand the line. The "smaller start" it offers is "one desk scoped in writing", with no number. The result chips "The Front Desk" and "The Booking Desk" look like buttons but are not among the tappable things. Severity: annoyance.

14. **Mailbox, "One message. I read every one."** The headline says he reads every one. The line under the send button says "Your message lands with The Ninja Agent, an AI ... It writes back within a minute". It is honest, but the first reply is a robot. After a want button jumps me to the form, the empty name field has a red ring that looks like an error. The floating "Take the quiz / Ask" bar covers the top of the send button and the line under it. Severity: cosmetic.

## Moments that worked

- **The chat box answered my first question in under a minute.** "Bookings on autopilot. No back and forth ... Reminders, reschedules and cancels handle themselves." Then the spa example: "each therapist's call-in notice, so it only offers a slot the room can actually hold." That IS my clinic. Keep that line.
- **Prices on screen 4 and 5**, plain, two cards, "Cancel anytime", "The AI is in the price." I did not have to hunt for a number.
- **"Your team checks it and presses send."** I knew at once who does what. How it works fits in four short lines.
- **The quiz respected my time.** Question 2 names "a clinic". Question 4 names my two pains exactly ("Answering the same questions, again and again" and "Scheduling. Does Tuesday work"). Twelve taps took about 90 seconds. The result named The Front Desk and The Booking Desk.
- **The quiz told me the truth against his own sale:** "by your own numbers that is about $1,300 a month back against $2,222 a month. It does not add up yet, and I would rather say so than sell it." That did more for my trust than anything else on the page.
- **Hand-off to the mailbox.** "Talk to me about it" carries all twelve answers and the quiz's sums into the message, with two blanks on top ("My company:", "What I would love off my plate:"). The Booking Desk's "I want this Tew" writes "I want this Tew: The Booking Desk." plus three blanks. Either way the form is two seconds away, and no email app opens.
- **The FAQ is folded into questions**, so I can skim it in one screen. "Can I get just one desk?" is exactly my question.
- **"Days, for the first desk"** (chat box) and "it drafts, you press": I can picture the first week.

## The moment I knew

**On the page:** in "Hate tech, tired of it, or no time for it. You still want it to work.", about **5 screens down** (of 42.5). By then I had seen the Your Dojo card ($2,222 a month, "Your team checks it and presses send", "The AI is in the price") and the line "The 'I want this Tew' button writes your pick into my mailbox", and How it works step 2 names "the bookings" at the top of screen 6. At that point I could say:

- what he sells: AI that drafts replies, files payments and takes bookings
- who does the computer work: he does, "Nothing for you to log into"
- what it costs: $2,222 a month
- what to do next: press the button, add a line, send

**In the chat box:** six questions and about three minutes. It was faster for "do you do bookings" and slower for the price, because of the 250-word cost answer.

**Still missing at that moment:**

- which plan covers my bookings (the $99 card mentions a "booking form")
- whether it works with the booking software I already pay for
- whether it does WhatsApp
- whether one desk costs less than $2,222

The page never fully answered the first three. The FAQ answered the last one, at screen 40.

**Where I would have left in real life:** at the top of the story, "Five months. Solutions and goals. One reason.", screen 8, about 5 to 6 minutes in (3 minutes in the chat box, a minute or two on the cards). From there I would have pressed the floating "Take the quiz", then "Talk to me about it", typed two lines and gone. I would never have seen screens 9 to 36. I kept reading in character to judge them.

3. **The Claudeforce band and its FAQ entry.** I never heard of it, and its numbers read as prices to someone flicking for prices.
4. **"Five words · five boxes",** the glossary.

6. **"Why I do this",** a repeat of the headline and the story.

It is the question that decides whether I write, and right now I have to write just to ask it.
