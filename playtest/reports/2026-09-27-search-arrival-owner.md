Read on a phone (Playwright, iPhone 13 profile, 390 x 664), live site. Start page:
https://ricktew.com/aininja/done-for-you/ , then its links into

## Verdict in one line
Maria took the quiz and would write one short question tonight (what does one desk cost on its own, and does it handle texts), but she would not sign up at $2,222. The search page convinced her in five seconds. The jump to the long page nearly lost her: all three buttons she pressed dropped her in the wrong spot. The quiz's honest math is what won her back.

## Moments of friction (the core of the report)

1. **[search page] Top bar, "Which ninja? / Take the HI Ninja door".** I expected the page I tapped in Google. The very first line on my screen, above the logo, asks me which ninja I want and offers a red "HI Ninja door". Did I land on the wrong site? What is HI? I had no idea and moved on. **Cosmetic.**

2. **[search page] Hero paragraph: "Your team checks the work and presses send."** I expected "done for you" to mean somebody else checks it. I have eleven cleaners and two vans. I *am* the office, so "your team" is me, at 9 pm, checking drafts again. That is exactly the babysitting I'm trying to escape. Lower down, the page even lists it as the other apps' fault: "Most AI tools hand you the tool and leave the rest with you: setting it up, learning it, **checking it**..." Then "Your press on every send: Drafts wait for your review." So checking is still my job. I get the safety reason, but in character it felt like the headline took back half of what it promised. **Annoyance** (my core doubt, and nothing on the page settles it).

3. **[search page] Plan cards, "BASIC PLAN · R2 Hosting, $222 to build, then $99 a month".** I expected "Basic" to be a smaller version of the admin help. It's a website. I don't need a website, I need my Thursday texts handled. So for my problem there's no basic plan, only $2,222. And "R2" means nothing to me. **Annoyance.**

4. **[search page] FAQ "Can I get just one desk?".** I didn't know what a desk was until I opened it. Once I did: one desk is one AI on one job, "and the price stays $2,222 a month, flat." For one job, that's far more than both my apps together. For an eleven-cleaner company that's a payroll-sized number. **Annoyance.**

Instead I'm sent to another page to write, and the first thing that answers me is another AI. That's honest, and he says a person follows up, but I came for the "real person" in the headline. **Annoyance.**

## Moments that worked

- **[search page] The headline matched my Google search word for word:** "Done-for-you AI for small business owners. Managed by a real person." I knew in under five seconds what this was and who it was for.
- **[search page]** "For owners who hate tech, are tired of it, or have no time for it." That's me on two of three.
- **[search page]** "Nothing to log into: No dashboard, no AI account, no key, no meter. When something needs changing, you tell me." This is the line that answers "is this another app I have to run?" It's the best line on either page for me. I only found it on screen five, not the first screen.
- **[search page]** "Most AI tools hand you the tool and leave the rest with you: setting it up, learning it, checking it, keeping up with which model is best this month." That describes my spring exactly.
- **[search page]** Short, calm, prices visible, "cancel anytime, effective at the end of the paid month", and an honest "Not in the price" line. Nine screens.
- **[main page]** "The one reason: I wanted my evenings back. If that is your reason too, this page is for you." That's my reason.
- **[main page]** "Friends asked about AI, then asked me to do it for them... 'could you just do it for us?'" That's me asking.
- **[main page]** The five-box storyboard's line "A ninja is one AI worker in it, doing one job." finally told me what a ninja is.
- **[main page, quiz]** "A trade. A garage, a plumber, a builder, a cleaner" named my trade. "Scheduling. Does Tuesday work, can we move it, are you free" is literally my Thursday texts.
- **[main page, quiz result]** "by your own numbers that is about $1,300 a month back against $2,222 a month. It does not add up yet, and I would rather say so than sell it." This made me trust him more than anything else on either page. Keep it exactly as it is.
- **[main page, chat box]** Misses are honest, and "Ask Rick this" put my question into the mailbox as "Your ninja on the page did not have this one: ..." with blanks for what I do. It landed right on the form.
- **[main page, mailbox]** It says plainly what happens: an AI answers within a minute and says it's an AI, then Rick follows up himself, usually within a day. The "I want this Tew" prefill on the Your Dojo card ("What I do / What eats the hours right now / When I would like it running") was easy to fill.
- **[main page, FAQ]** "What happens if you disappear?" said my domain, content and customer records leave with me and he keeps no copy.

## Did I understand what is sold, what it costs, and what happens next?

- **What is sold: YES.** The search page says it: "I build it, host it and manage the AI ninjas that do the legwork: AI workers with one job each, drafting your replies, filing your payment mail, keeping your bookings." The gap for me is that I still review and press every send, and that it works on email, not texts, out of the box.
- **What it costs: YES for the two plans, NO for my size.** "$2,222 / month, flat" for the admin help, and "$222 to build, then $99 a month" for a website. The one option that fits an eleven-cleaner company, one desk on its own, is "a custom job, quoted in writing". Having it run for me is "a custom price, and we talk first". Neither has a number.
- **What happens next: YES, mostly.** "It writes back within a minute... and it always says it is an AI. I read every message and follow up myself, usually within a day." After that comes the free audit, then the first ninja "up and running within days" (chat box). The gap: how the audit is done (in writing or a call?) and what access to my email or phone he would need.

## The one change

Keep Maria on the short page when she decides to write. The search page's closing line, "My mailbox is on the AI Ninja page.", sends her to a 72-screen page, and all three jump buttons land her above the target, on "Pricing an agent can read". Put the mailbox (or the quiz entry) on /aininja/done-for-you/ itself, or at minimum make those jumps land on the form and the quiz. The search page already convinced her. The jump is where she almost left.
