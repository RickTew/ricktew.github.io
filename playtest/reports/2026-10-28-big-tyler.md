# Big round, 28 Oct 2026: Tyler (24, gamer, never trained, phone, Instagram ad)

iPhone 13 only, live page, nothing sent or paid.

## Verdict in one line

The Ninja Menu and the gamer stuff hooked me hard, but I'd write before paying $2,222 because I can't tell who actually teaches my two weeks, what the other beginners look like, or what happens to my money if it's not for me.

## First 10 seconds

"Martial Arts / Ninja Course / by Rick Tew" plus "Koh Samui Island, Thailand." Ninja and Thailand, my two things, so I stayed. The photo (three people in fighting stance by a red punching bag) is a little "gym guy" for me; I guessed blue polo is Rick. It wants Book Now. I wasn't ready, but "What do you want to learn? Pick 5 tactics" is what I'd press. "HI Ninja" means nothing to me (Hawaii? hello?).

## Would I book

Ask first. The stay I'd want is 1 week at $1,111, to test it inside my three-week trip, but I only found it in small print under "Every stay." Second choice: 2 weeks at $2,222, if a buddy could come free in November.

What stops me:
- "Rick often rotates between the morning and evening sessions when he is available." The page says "by Rick Tew," so is Rick my teacher or not?
- "All sales are final." This is most of my trip money.
- No recent, dated photo of an actual beginner group on Samui.

To book, I'd need one honest line on who runs the beginner classes and how many people are in them, plus a recent picture of a normal-looking group.

## The photos

Mostly yes. Convinced me: the 8-tile class grid, the young guy with the nunchaku, the woman kicking the bag low, the gamer bunk room (sold me more than any kick), Rick's jump kick over the bay, the student quote faces, and "From 13, a wannabe ninja in the backyard" in the Rick video. That's me at ten.

Off for me: many look 2010s (old colour, a letterboxed old video), almost everyone is fit and in black uniforms, nobody out of shape. Someone like me? Young guys, yes. My size and fitness, no.

The changing boxes helped: more places and people than I'd scroll through. But one froze mid-fade into a ghost double exposure, and I saw no dots or arrows to go back to one I liked.

## The bonuses

**WinJitsu:** Rick's mind training (focus, goals, beating self-doubt), coaching with him, five books, and a free month of an app.

**Pocket Dojo:** the belt lessons on my phone, so I can keep training after I fly home.

Both say "BONUS ... WITH EVERY STAY," so I read them as included. But:
- The books "join your belts from orange on," so not on two weeks? Then why list them for every stay?
- The WinJitsu box is long (Pai story, quote) and reads like a motivational-speaker pitch. I skimmed it.
- Pocket Dojo ends with a big red "SEE THE HOME STUDY PROGRAM" button: looks like a product for sale.
- Nothing says how long I keep Pocket Dojo, or if I get it BEFORE I arrive, which would fix my "worst in the room" fear.

Pocket Dojo made the price feel a bit more worth it; WinJitsu, neutral.

## Welcome

Yes: "GAMERS AND NERDS: Nerdy ninja conversations and a game room." "Not a fight camp. No sparring unless you ask." "Not a boot camp." "Built for beginners." "Any level can pick any technique." The Gaming Zone (Xbox with Game Pass, Switch on 75-inch screens). NerdHostel.

No: all-fit photos; the Belt, Mastery and Instructor sections (feels like it's for serious people); and "Got an injury, or not very fit? Tell me before you come," one line that never touches being shy or the worst in the group.

## The chat

**Asked:** "I'm super out of shape and never trained. Will I be the worst one there?"

**Got:** "More than one answer fits that. Which one did you mean?" plus two buttons. I tapped "Got an injury, or not very fit?": "Tell me before you come. Every drill has an easier version." Fast, but it felt like a menu bot and dodged the "worst one there" part.

**Also asked:** "is there sparring? i dont want to get punched"

**Got:** "Not a fight camp. No sparring unless you ask. Not a boot camp..." Perfect.

## Legit or gimmick

Rick is real, and so is the training history. I decided at the photo boxes, then the press list (Black Belt magazine) and the backyard-ninja video.

What I'm unsure about is whether this two-week program, as written, is proven: the next group is 22 January 2027, NerdHostel's own site says "Opens 10 Nov 2026," and "when he is available." "Only 12 spots left," "save $2,000 in November" and "friend free in November" stacked together set off my scam radar.

**Vote: UNSURE.**

## Moments that worked

- The Ninja Menu is a skill-tree loadout: belt-coloured picks, "ADDED," "Any level can pick any technique." I picked Sword, Nunchaku, Blinding Powder, Forward Roll and Front Kicks, and "Your Ninja Week is ready" felt great. Keep it.
- Gamers and Nerds, the Gaming Zone, the NerdHostel bunk photo.
- "Not a fight camp / Not a boot camp."
- A clear day: 8 to 10 am, optional evening, free weekends, airport pick-up, bed included.
- "That was then. Your intro is the easy-going version."
- Student quotes with faces. The sparring chat answer. Ninjitsu vs ninjutsu explained.

## Words I did not understand

HI Ninja; "private group workshop" (private AND group?); PSC; RTMS; "Mini-Martial Art System via Stances"; "Combat Reality"; "Natural Reactions"; Guide (capital G); "Strategic Intervention Coach, Robbins-Madanes"; Tew's Tour; Pocket Dojo vs Home Study Program (two names, one thing?).

## Buy, write or leave

Write. I'd use the question box: Who runs the beginner mornings? How many people are usually in a group, and how fit are they? Can I get Pocket Dojo before I arrive? Can I do 1 week first?

## The one change

Near the price, show a recent, dated photo or clip of an actual beginner group on Samui with normal bodies, plus one plain line on who teaches it. That answers "is it real" and "will I be the worst" in one shot.
