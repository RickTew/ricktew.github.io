# Henrik, 54, Copenhagen: /hininja/ (28 Oct 2026, big round)

## Verdict in one line

The teacher is real and the 2-month belt fits my six months, but the page sells a relaxed holiday for young gamers and never says who I would train beside, so I would write before I pay.

## First 10 seconds

A martial arts course on Koh Samui, taught by one named man, Rick Tew. The photo looks real, not stock. For whom: "go-at-your-own-pace" says anyone; "Ninja Course" and "HI Ninja" (I do not know what HI means) say young men. It wants me to press Book Now (twice on one phone screen) or pick 5 tactics. I would press neither. The phone has no menu, so I scrolled; on the laptop I would press "RICK" first.

## Would I book

Not yet; I would ask first, through the question box. My stay: 2 months, $3,997, the belt program, "For the serious", "earned in a test with Rick". That is the Camino shape I want: a hard thing with an end. Two weeks reads as a holiday ("plenty of time for the island").

What stops me:
- How does a belt-program day differ from the two-hour morning seminar? Ten hours a week, "relaxed", "the easy-going version", is not the discipline I came for.
- Nobody states the age of a typical group.
- "All sales are final", for a 54-year-old who may strain something in week two.

To book I need: hours per day on the belt, what the yellow belt test involves, and the honest age range of past groups.

## The photos

They make it real; none look like stock. Convincing: Rick and a middle-aged man with sticks on a terrace above the sea; the group in guard stance among eucalyptus; above all the older man in the yellow shirt holding a training knife. He is the only person my age I found, and only because I waited: on my first scroll the box captioned "Every age, every level" showed a man of about thirty. The older man is one picture of nine in that box.

Off: the eight-photo grid near the top looks fifteen years old, all young people; a man rolling over a plastic chair looks like a back injury; the bunk-bed dorm looks like a youth hostel. The changing boxes showed variety, but the one picture meant for me is hidden in a carousel.

## The bonuses

WinJitsu: Rick's mental training for focus, goals and self-doubt; coaching with him outside class, five books, a free month of an app. Pocket Dojo: a phone app with the belt syllabus, foot diagrams, a "practiced" tick and a box to ask Rick. Both say "with every stay", so I read them as bonuses, and the WinJitsu coaching raised the value for me: a clear head is half of why I would go. But "They join your belts from orange on" suggests a yellow belt gets no books; Pocket Dojo ends with a red "See the Home Study Program" button that looks like a separate purchase; the "Every stay" list under the prices omits Pocket Dojo; no access length is given. Net: slightly less trust.

## Welcome

Yes: "16 and up. No upper age limit." "Am I too old? No." "Not a boot camp. Every drill has an easier version." "Tell me about an old injury before you come." The older man with the knife.

No: "Gamers and nerds", "Nerdy ninja conversations, a game room", a race car simulator, Nintendo on 75-inch screens, the cartoon assistant, a shared dorm with no private room, "Attend with a friend for free". I would be allowed in; I am not sure I would fit.

## The chat

I asked: "I am 54 and have never done a martial art. Am I too old, and will everyone else in the group be in their twenties?" It gave the FAQ's "Am I too old?" word for word and ignored the second half, my real question. "What is the usual age of the people in the group?" got "I do not have that one written down" and an offer to send it. Honest, not helpful. I did not send.

## Legit or gimmick

**LEGIT**, on the man. I decided at the two-minute video under "Who you learn from": on TV at 15, teaching outdoors at Naarden Vesting (a Dutch fortress I know), live-in camps in California, then Thailand. The Black Belt magazine mention helped. What cost trust: "Only 12 spots left for 2027", "save $2,000", the unprovable "first in the martial arts industry", quotes signed with initials and no year, and "The history" page, an older site whose banner reads "KIDS Martial Arts at NinjaGym". Is the centre a children's gym?

## Moments that worked

- "Not a fight camp. No sparring unless you ask. Not a boot camp."
- The teaching timeline video.
- The older man in the yellow shirt.
- The plain schedule: 8 to 10 morning, optional 8 to 10 evening, weekends free.
- The yellow belt syllabus spelled out (Horse, Cat, Palm Heel). Concrete.
- Prices shown; dates can move within a year; free airport pick-up; the free 20-page PDF.
- The laptop layout: calm, readable, a proper top menu.

## Words I did not understand

HI Ninja; "tactics" (I would say topics); Sensei Seminar; RTMS; PSC Self-Defense Program; Mini-Martial Art System via Stances; WinJitsu; Pocket Dojo; Guide (capital G); Ninja Menu; Tew's Tour; Tonfa; Strategic Intervention Coach; "Ninjitsu (most people spell it ninjutsu)", which reads like a misspelling defended.

## Buy, write or leave

Write, through the question box, topic "The training and the belts": hours per day on the 2-month belt, what the yellow belt test involves, the age range of a typical group, and help finding a private room nearby.

## The one change

Say who is in the room. Put the older student first in the "Every age" box, and state the real age range of past groups in Who, in the FAQ "Am I too old?" and in the chat.
