# Marco, the skeptical buyer - 2026-08-30

Session: desktop Chrome, cold arrival (storage cleared), about 35 minutes in
character. Read the whole page top to bottom, opened every drawer and FAQ,
used the chat box (seven questions), did all twelve quiz questions, filled the
mailbox and did not send it, read Terms of Service and Privacy. The browser
tooling would not open outside domains, so the one thing Marco would have done
next (open ninjagym.com to see the "live" gym app) could not be done from
here; it also could not be done from the page, see friction 3.

## Verdict in one line
Marco would take the quiz (he did), read the Terms (he did), and then close
the tab without writing: three editor's notes left in the live copy, no build
he can open, a seat count that three places describe three different ways, and
the page's own quiz math telling him the service costs twice what it would
save him. He would keep the Terms page bookmarked, which is more than most
pages get from him.

## Moments that worked
- The two price cards. Price, what is in, "NOT INCLUDED" with three lines,
  "Monthly subscription. Cancel anytime, effective at the end of the paid
  month." The same cancel sentence appears on both cards, in the FAQ, in the
  chat box and in the Terms, identical every time. That consistency is rare
  and Marco noticed it.
- Ownership. "The build sits in your own accounts. Cancel and it stays"
  (Claudeforce band), "everything built in your accounts stays yours; it was
  always in your name" (FAQ), Terms section 4 "Your accounts stay yours". Same
  claim, four places, no drift.
- "Honest line: if three of you need a contact list and nothing else, buy
  their Starter at $25 a seat. I will not beat that." A seller telling me when
  to buy the competitor. Marco read that twice.
- "No testimonials yet, on purpose... I will not invent a quote to fill a
  box." Better than three fake quotes, and the asked/shipped pairs are
  specific ($5 per staff PIN, a 1998 factory).
- The quiz. Plain questions, no email wall, "Honest math" printed under the
  result, the result matched the answers, and "Talk to me about it" wrote my
  exact answers into the mailbox as "Quiz results" with every question and
  the answer I chose. That prefill said what I meant.
- The chat box saying "I do not have that one, and I would rather say so
  than guess." Marco trusts a box that admits a miss.
- "Every send, pay and post waits for your press." Said many times, never
  contradicted, backed by the Terms section 5.
- The Terms themselves: plain language, Stripe, monthly in advance, a
  liability cap, a named company and state (Tew's Inc, Las Vegas, Nevada),
  a date. Marco reads terms and these are the best part of the site.
- The POS story: "$5 per staff member per month just to enter a PIN". A
  concrete grievance Marco has had himself.
- "I'm not from that playbook." The paragraph about seminar speakers is
  funny and Marco agreed with it, which is why the leaked notes hurt more.

## Did I understand what is sold, what it costs, and what happens next?
- What is sold: YES, eventually. "You are hiring my service to build and run
  your AI workforce, not me as your product developer" (the paragraph under
  the price cards) and Terms section 1. It took 22,000 pixels to reach it.
- What it costs: YES for the subscription, NO for the whole bill. "$4,444 /
  month" and "$2,222 / month" are stated the same way on the cards, in the
  FAQ, in the chat box, in the quiz result, in the mailbox prefill and in the
  Terms. The gaps: third-party AI and software costs (no estimate anywhere),
  and the build-work price ($222 per 2-hour block, $2,222 for 20 hours) that
  exists only in the Terms while the page says "scoped in writing first".
- What happens next: PARTLY. The FAQ says "The audit first: we map where your
  hours actually go, together. Then your first ninja... at white belt." The
  mailbox says an AI answers within a minute and Rick follows up. What is not
  said: how the audit is booked, how long it takes, whether it is inside the
  first $2,222 or before it, and when the first invoice lands.
