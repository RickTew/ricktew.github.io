# Ruth, small law office partner - 2026-10-01 (round seven)

Read headless at 1280 x 800 (laptop), cold, starting at https://ricktew.com/aininja/. The page is 14,347 px tall, about 18 screens. About 20 minutes in character. I opened every folded answer in both FAQ lists, the Front Desk row, the five-words paragraph, the Terms and the Privacy page, asked the chat box seven questions, and filled the mailbox from the Your Dojo card without sending it. I did not take the quiz.

Safety: the two mail endpoints (ricktew-contact, ricktew-intake) were blocked in every script before any page load. The block never fired, and a request listener on the mailbox run saw zero calls to any function. Nothing was sent.

## Verdict in one line
I would write one guarded due-diligence question, not a request to buy: the offer, the price and the next step were clear by screen 2.5, but who can read privileged client mail is not answered anywhere on the page itself, only in two sentences of the Terms and in the chat box.

## Moments of friction (the core of the report)

1. **Top screen** ("I'm not a marketing expert. I'm the person behind your AI ninjas."). About 30 seconds to form "one man builds an AI admin setup for a business and helps run it", which matches what my colleague said. What "the Dojo" actually is, I could not have said. The "I want this Tew!!" buttons read as unserious to me. Recorded only, as instructed. **Cosmetic.**
2. **Number strip just under the top screen.** "30+ YEARS A NINJA", "25+ YEARS A NERD", "6,242+ ROUNDS OF CHANGES SINCE MARCH". The last one, to a lawyer, reads as "this has been changed six thousand times in seven months", which is the opposite of stable. **Annoyance.**
3. **"Hate tech, tired of it, or no time for it."** The checklist is not me. I do not hate technology; my problem is volume and a duty of confidentiality. I kept reading only because the price cards appeared right under it. **Cosmetic.**
4. **The price cards.** "BASIC PLAN · R2 HOSTING": R2 meant nothing until the small grey print, "R2 is me, Rick Tew." **Cosmetic.**
5. **Card versus How it works.** The Your Dojo card says "Your team checks it and presses send." Step 3 of How it works says "A person checks it." Which person, his or mine? With privileged mail, that is the whole question. **Annoyance.**
6. **"Is it safe to let an AI read my inbox?"** (folded, first FAQ). It tells me what the AI does ("Attachments are never opened", "every draft it ever made stays on file"), which is good. It does not tell me who on Rick's side can read my clients' mail, which AI company processes it, whether that company keeps it, or where it is stored. It has no link to the Terms. The word "confidential" appears nowhere on the page or in any folded answer. Had the Terms not been in the footer, I would have closed the tab here. **Would-quit.**
7. **"FIVE WORDS · FIVE BOXES."** A cartoon vocabulary lesson, which I needed only because the page invents its own words. "A seat is a job a few ninjas fill together" is defined and then never used again anywhere on the page. **Annoyance.**
8. **"Five months. Solutions and goals. One reason."** The headline reads as a riddle. Below it: a kids' gym on Koh Samui and "I am a Ninja Nerd." I skimmed it. **Annoyance.**
9. **"Just a few of the apps I've made recently."** Games ("Dungeon Hole", "AstroHold", "Ninja Ninja Defense: Only a Ninja can defeat a Ninja"), a hostel, "TEWBEDO overseer access only", and then the same list again as a row of icons. I would not put client mail near something I first met beside a ninja video game. **This is where I would have stopped reading in real life** and jumped to the footer for the Terms. **Would-quit.**
10. **"The website is not the machine. It is the handle."** A spa app, a till, "Inventory that counts itself", "Shop two is turn-key": none of it is a law office. Worse, it ends with "The website is the ten percent above the water. Buy only that and you bought a brochure." Two screens earlier the Basic plan offered me exactly that, a website, for $222. So which is it? **Annoyance (LOST).**
11. **"The solutions. Every one ran on me first."** The band above it says "ELEVEN, EACH PROVEN ON MY OWN BUSINESS." Then The Video Room says "Built, in human testing now", and The Publishing House runs "in a real publishing house", which is a friend's. A claim that does not hold on its own page is the kind of thing I note in a contract. **Annoyance.**
12. **"Your own Digital Dojo. Built by a sensei."** "Mine start at white belt: propose only, prove it, earn the next belt. Nothing sends, pays or posts itself until it has." So once a ninja has earned the belt, it does send by itself? Every other place says the opposite: "A person presses every send. That rule never bends" (same section, first card) and "That rule does not bend at any belt or any price" (second FAQ). On the one rule I care about most, the page contradicts itself. **Would-quit** (I had already left in real life).
13. **Same section.** "You run the business. I run the ninjas that do your admin." sits a few lines under "I help you run it; I do not run it for you." I can reconcile them, but I should not have to. Then "fill in The Intake: 20 to 40 minutes, a voice note and a short video." A video of myself, for a stranger, before I have seen a contract? No. **Annoyance.**
14. **"Twelve questions. Then you tell me if I'm wrong."** "The belt bar fills as you go, white through black." The first question offers "Nothing yet. I work a job, and I am looking", which tells me this page is for everyone, not for a practice like mine. Did not take it. **Cosmetic.**
15. **"Why I do this."** The friend who said "just do it for us" and "AI to HI" again, for the third time. **Annoyance.**
16. **"The questions that come up every time."** This is the second FAQ list on the page, and neither list asks "Who can see my clients' information?" "Does anyone test it before my customers see it?" answers "Simulated players go first", and "players" is an odd word for a law firm's mail. **Cosmetic.**
17. **"One message. I read every one."** The note under the form says the first reply comes from "The Ninja Agent, an AI I built". For a confidential enquiry, the first reader is an AI. I can live with that because it is disclosed, but I will write nothing about a client. "my own address is not written on this page for the crawlers to eat" is cute, and I do not want cute. **Annoyance.**
18. **Mailbox prefill.** I pressed "I want this Tew" on the Your Dojo card. The subject came up as "Which fits me, Your Dojo or R2 Hosting?" even though I had just picked Your Dojo. **Cosmetic.**
19. **Finding the Terms.** The only link on the page is in the footer, in small type, on the last of 18 screens ("Terms of Service · Privacy"). The price cards do not link them, and neither does the safety FAQ. The one other route is the chat box's "Read the Terms" button. **Annoyance.**
20. **Inside the Terms.** They are plain, dated (14 September 2026) and readable, which I respect. But section 7, Confidentiality, is two sentences, and it confirms that Rick sees my business. No AI provider or sub-processor is named, and no storage location is given. Section 8 caps liability at three months' fees, about $6,666. A privilege breach would cost far more than that. Section 1 has a price list that is not on the offer page: "$222 per 2-hour block, or $2,222 for a 20-hour package". So "$2,222" means two different things on the same site. **Annoyance.**
21. **Chat box, "Which AI company processes the messages, and do they keep or train on them?"** It answered "Which AI do you use?" with "I do not carve a name into this page", and the half about keeping or training was ignored entirely. For a law firm, the processor of our mail is not a detail. **Annoyance** (I would ask it in writing).

## Moments that worked
- **The two price cards at screen 2.** "$2,222 a month, flat", "Any number of people", "The AI is in the price", plus the cancel terms. I knew the cost of my whole firm before I had read 600 words.
- "Your team checks it and presses send." and "Nothing goes out without a press." That is the right answer for privileged mail, when it is said consistently.
- "I stick to text on purpose: everything we agree is on the record." A lawyer likes exactly this.
- **"What happens if you disappear?"** "your domain, your mail, your content, your videos, your customer records. They are yours in writing, they leave with you, and I keep no copy." Clear, and a promise I could hold him to.
- **The safety FAQ, in part:** "Attachments are never opened", "it cannot touch money, and every draft it ever made stays on file." That is an audit trail.
- "The people stay unnamed on purpose." Discretion about his clients makes me trust him more with mine.
- **The chat box shows which question it answered** (a heading above each reply), so I could see when it had substituted a nearby question. Its honest misses ("I do not have that one, and I would rather say so than guess") are better than a bluff.
- **Chat, NDA:** "If you need your own NDA on top, send it with your message." And: "If your industry has a specific rule it must meet, say so in the mailbox and I will tell you straight whether the Dojo meets it." Those two lines are why I would write at all.
- The chat box's hand-off wrote all seven of my questions into the mailbox for me. That saves retyping, and it shows him what I asked.
- **The Terms themselves:** plain language, dated, and section 5, "the judgment calls are never Rick's to make on your behalf." The Privacy page says no tracking.

## The moment I knew
**"Hate tech, tired of it, or no time for it. You still want it to work."**, at the two short paragraphs under the price cards, about 2.5 screens down (of 18), roughly 90 seconds in. By then I had all four:
- **What he sells:** "your admin handled as a service. I manage the AI ninjas that draft every reply and file every payment."
- **Who does the computer work:** he does; my team presses send.
- **What it costs my firm:** "$2,222 a month, flat ... Any number of people."
- **What to do next:** "The 'I want this Tew' button writes your pick into my mailbox at the bottom of this page. You add a line and send it." The audit comes first and is free.

Still missing at that moment: who on his side can read my clients' mail, which AI company processes it and whether it keeps it, where the data is stored, a link to the Terms, and any sign that a professional office (rather than a gym, a spa or a shop) fits.

## Would I write?
Yes, guardedly: one written due-diligence question, with no client details in it, because a colleague vouched for him, the price fits a five-person office, and the chat box said to send our own NDA. The first line I would type into "WHERE YOUR HOURS GO":

"We are a three-lawyer family law practice with two paralegals; before anything else I need, in writing, which AI provider would process our client email, where that data is stored, and who on your side can read it."

## The one change
Put a plain answer to "Who can see my clients' mail?" next to the price cards and inside "Is it safe to let an AI read my inbox?": who on Rick's side can read it, which AI provider processes it and whether that provider keeps it, where it is stored, and a link to the Terms (section 7). For a law office, that one answer decides whether the rest of the page gets read.
