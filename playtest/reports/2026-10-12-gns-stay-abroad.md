# GNS round, 12 Oct 2026: Lena, the long-stayer on Koh Samui (stay-abroad)

Start: https://ricktew.com/hininja/, phone then laptop, from a Samui expats Facebook post.

## Verdict in one line

Real training with an honest schedule, but neither page talks to someone who already lives on Samui and only wants the classes, so I would write first and probably leave over the price.

## Legit or gimmick

The HI page felt real within 10 seconds: "about 2 hours of training a day ... Not crazy serious like the camps of the old days." I decided it was legit at "What it is" (stances, rolls, falls, self-defense), the Field record cards and the photo of Rick teaching. What almost tipped it: on this plain page, "The goofy is the whole point. Every session is silly", then "THE AMERICAN NINJA" and the Tony Robbins credential, which reads as salesy self-help to me.

The GNS page shows a cartoon mascot, "Zero seriousness", and no real class photo outside the sealed file. It looks like a party hostel. The belt tabs (rolls, falls, back kick) helped.

**Votes: HI page LEGIT. GNS page UNSURE (LEGIT once I opened the files).**

## The files

I saw the big orange "Declassify the file" button and would not have pressed it: it reads as a marketing trick, and I was scrolling for the price. Most practical people like me would skip it.

Inside: the first tape, TRMS 2000, opens on a cartoon logo, then grainy year-2000 kicks in a field. That is history, not a class today. What changed my mind was the Asia file, slide 2, "Inside NinjaGym on Koh Samui": adult men and women training on red mats. People like me. The Europe file (six years in Holland, TU Delft) made him feel closer to home.

**Make one thing visible:** that Asia photo grid, on the HI page under "What it is", or in place of the jump-kick hero.

## Which page

HI. Plain, the day laid out, the FAQ answers real questions. GNS is built for young gamers ("gaming dojo: RPGs, LAN").

**Vote: HI.**

## Words I did not understand

HI Ninja (on its own page), RTMS / TRMS, Ninjitsu, Ninja Tools, club-style review, NinjaGym Guide, Strategic Intervention Coach, "the 222 stays", "I want this Tew", Digital Dojo, Pocket Dojo, Ninja code WVXB, Twilight Strategist, Coffee and Kicks, C.A.N.U.

## Who it is for, and not for

GNS makes me picture twenty-something backpackers with a console. HI says "Beginners to experienced", "seasoned fighters". The Asia photos show mixed adults, which calmed me. I do not expect gym bros or Muay Thai fighters, but nothing rules them out, and nothing says who it is NOT for. One line would help: "Not a fight camp, no sparring unless you want it", plus whether residents join.

## How I would have found it

The Samui expats Facebook group, a Lamai coworking noticeboard, or a friend. On Google: "adult martial arts classes Koh Samui", "self defense class women Samui", "beginner martial arts Lamai". An ad that stops me: "Live on Samui? Adult ninja classes at Big C, evenings, beginners welcome, not a fight gym", with a real photo of adults on the mats.

## What I would tell a friend

"A relaxed holiday with training, not a hard camp. Weekdays, a 2-hour class 8 to 10am plus an optional 2-hour evening review. Rick Tew teaches at least two classes a week, a gym guide the rest. NinjaGym at Big C. Two weeks is $2,222 with a hostel bunk and meals, $1,000 per extra week. For travellers who want real moves and the island too."

## Buy, write or leave

Write first, from the HI form: a resident, evenings-only price? Is 22 January a fixed start? Can my brother join one week in February? To buy I need a resident answer on the page, a current class photo, a map pin and clear dates. At $2,222 for a bunk I will not sleep in, I leave.

## The one change

In the HI price card, add one line for people who already have a place on Samui, with the honest answer from the quiz (same price, or a class-only price), beside a current photo of adults training at the gym.
