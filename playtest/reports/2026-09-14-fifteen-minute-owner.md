## Verdict in one line
She would not write to Rick today and she would not finish the quiz: the chat box told her yes to bookings, yes to reply drafting and $2,222 a month inside three minutes, which is exactly what she asked for, but the price was double what the quiz later said her problem was worth, WhatsApp (where her bookings actually arrive) is "a build, not a switch", nobody would tell her a start date, and the twelve taps ran out her clock. ## Moments that worked

- The chat box answered bookings in under a minute with "Bookings on autopilot. No back and forth." and the word "therapist". For this persona that one answer is the reason the tab stayed open.
- "Answers Rick wrote. No guessing." in the chat header, and then it did exactly that on Cliniko: "I do not have that one, and I would rather say so than guess. Rick does: ask him directly. It lands with his Ninja Agent, and he replies himself, usually within a day." with an "Ask Rick this" button. I believed it, and "usually within a day" is the only time promise on the page.
- The solution rows: one name, one line, a plus. "The Front Desk  Support replies drafted on auto. You press send." Expanded: "An AI reads every support mail, day and night, and drafts the reply from your own written answers. You read, you press send. Runs in my business today." That is the whole pitch in three sentences. "The Booking Desk" expanded is the same length.
- "You press every send" and "nothing goes out without a human press" are repeated everywhere, and for a clinic that is the first thing I would have asked. It was answered before I asked.
- The price card line "flat, any number of seats, the AI included" and "Monthly subscription. Cancel anytime, effective at the end of the paid month." Plain, and it answered my four-therapists question.
- The quiz result doing the sum against me: "It does not add up yet, and I would rather say so than sell it." Nobody selling anything says that. It is the line I would quote to my colleague.
- The quiz's second question lists "a clinic" in the first option, and the result named exactly the two rows I had opened. It read me right.
- The Tew Tips are short and practical: "Count the messages it takes to book one slot today. That number is what disappears." I did, in my head. It is about forty.
- The tick-list under "You do not like tech": "You have no time to learn it, and none to fix it." That is me.
- The "I want this Tew" pill after every chat answer, and the mailbox prefilled with my quiz answers, so whoever reads it knows what I asked without me retyping it.
