# Henrik, 54, Copenhagen: head-to-head, 21 Oct 2026

## Verdict in one line
/hininja/: the only one I could read without feeling laughed at, but I would write first, not buy, because neither page shows a beginner of my age belongs in that room.

## First 10 seconds
**ricktew.com/hininja/** (phone, then laptop): two weeks of live-in ninja training on Koh Samui with a named teacher. Serious layout. Then the second paragraph: "It is relaxed... Not crazy serious like the camps of the old days", and under the jump-kick photo "Train a little, see the island a lot." So: a martial arts holiday.

**ricktew.com/gns/** (laptop): a cartoon ninja, "SHH, DON'T TELL ANYONE", "ZERO SERIOUSNESS", a ticker, emoji. A joke product or a party hostel. I nearly closed it.

## Legit or gimmick
- **/hininja/: UNSURE.** It says "The training is legit" but never shows it. Two photos, both of Rick alone; no mats, students, hostel or film. The credentials list ("first in the martial arts industry to run live-in camps", the magazine names) has no dates or sources I can check.
- **/gns/: LEGIT** for the training, decided late. The photo grid "Inside NinjaGym: red mats, real training" was the first sign of real adults. I was convinced in the sealed file: the 2000 tape (0:47, a man doing push-ups in mud; 3:22, groundwork on mats) and the Europe file: six years in Holland, lectures at TU Delft, talks at HP. Those I can check. I only opened it because I check everyone.

## The same thing?
Yes, on screen two of /hininja/: the "Our Gooffy version" button, then "Its name is Gooffy Ninja ShhT!!". Not confusing, but deflating: the serious page is the silly programme with a straight face.

## The price as I understood it
$2,222 (about 15,000 kroner) for two weeks: a bunk in a shared hostel dorm, "simple, hearty" meals (how many a day?), a taught class 8 to 10, Monday to Friday, and an optional 2-hour evening review. So 20 taught hours, 40 if I attend every evening. Rick teaches "at least 2 classes a week"; an unnamed Guide teaches the rest. Weekends, airport, insurance: not stated. Extra weeks $1,000. My own room nearby costs the same, plus a 1-on-1 with Rick. All sales final; dates can move to another two weeks within a year. Money is not my problem, but $2,222 for a bunk and mostly not-Rick teaching in a mall gym feels high; I would pay more for a private room and more Rick.

## My own question
Am I too old and stiff, and will it be twenty-year-olds? Neither page mentions age. /hininja/ comes closest in the FAQ "Got an injury, or not very fit?": "Every drill has an easier version, and the Guide keeps it at your level." Helpful but generic; /gns/ lacks it. Meanwhile "game by night", "RPGs, LAN", the free tee and the quiz describe a young crowd. Not answered.

My second question, is this something hard?, was answered, and the answer was no. Both pages apologise for "the camps of the old days", which are exactly what I want.

## Which page I would book from
**Vote: /hininja/.** "It is the one I can read and forward to a friend without apologising, but it still has to tell me a 54-year-old beginner fits in." I would forward /hininja/.

## What would make the other page win
/gns/ already has the better proof. One plain FAQ answer, no joke, "I am over 50 and stiff. Is this for me?", with the injury answer /hininja/ has, would make me book from /gns/.
