# Big round, 28 Oct 2026: Ken (46, Seattle history teacher, ninjutsu-curious skeptic)

Phone first, then a 1280 x 800 laptop (how Ken reads). Three videos scrubbed, all folds opened, chat asked twice, the 2-week card followed to the name and email form.

## Verdict in one line

The man is real and the old training tape sold me, but the page never says who taught Rick, the chat dodged when I asked, and "all five belts in a year, the fast way" plus a blank instructor certificate smells like the belt mills I have spent 30 years avoiding.

## First 10 seconds

"Martial Arts / Ninja Course / by Rick Tew", a real photo of a class on red mats, and a box asking me to pick 5 tactics. An in-person ninja course on Koh Samui for beginners and travelers; it wants me to press Book now or play the menu. I would scroll for the teacher instead. "HI Ninja" means nothing to me.

## Would I book

Ask first, through the question box. I would pick **2 weeks, $2,222, in July** (my summer break; "start any day from 15 January" misses my winter break). Three things stop me from booking now:

1. **Lineage.** "Rick's own system, Modern American Ninjitsu" is honest; no fake 34th-generation Japanese line. But nowhere does it say who HE trained under before building his own. The free PDF says "a background in forms such as Ninjitsu, Kickboxing, and Tae kwon do", no names.
2. **How much is Rick.** The seminar is one "Rick builds", and he "often rotates ... when he is available". Who teaches when he is not? How many of my 20 morning hours are his?
3. **The fast certificate.** See friction 3.

To book I need one paragraph on Rick's own teachers and ranks, and one line on how often he teaches the morning seminar himself.

## The photos

They make it real. What convinced me:
- **The old VHS tape** ("Curious what ninja training looks like?"): students climbing ropes up a gym wall, a class in black uniforms with real colored belts. Nobody fakes 1990s tracking lines.
- **"Sensei Tew about to perform a basic table roll"** in a coffee shop. That is my kind of joke on top of real skill.
- The open-air Thai hall with a green roof, a man in a blue uniform and black belt correcting a student's arm (the rotating hero). Teaching, not posing.
- The student quote cards with faces and names.

Off or old: most photos are low-resolution, from many years and countries, so I cannot tell which room I would train in. The Grand Canyon jump looks like a 90s magazine stunt; fun, but I squinted. The dorm photo is clearly current.

Someone like me: yes, the older man in a neon shirt with a training knife, men in their 40s in the hall. Rotating boxes: watching 10 seconds gave three varied scenes per box, which helped; six changing at once while I read captions was a little busy.

## The bonuses

**WinJitsu:** Rick's "mental martial art" for focus and goals: coaching, five self-help books from orange belt on, a free month of an app. A bonus, but a motivational-speaker one; beside "Robbins-Madanes" coaching it nudges me toward "life coach", away from "ninja". The treehouse desk is charming. Price feels the same.

**Pocket Dojo:** an app with the belt curriculum, stance footprint diagrams and "Ask Sensei Tew" per lesson, so I keep training in Seattle. That makes $2,222 feel more worth it. But the "Every stay" list and the friend promo do not mention it, it never says for how long, and the box ends in a red button selling the Home Study Program. Free, or an upsell?

## Welcome

Yes for a 46-year-old with three months of college aikido:
- "Am I too old? No. There is no upper age limit."
- "Not a fight camp. No sparring unless you ask." and "Not a boot camp. Every drill has an easier version."
- "Gamers and nerds: Nerdy ninja conversations and a game room." I run D&D on weekends; that line is aimed straight at me.
- "That was then. Your intro is the easy-going version." Under-promising, which I like.

Less welcome: a bunk-bed dorm at 46; the FAQ lets me book my own room.

## The chat

Asked: "Who did Rick learn ninjutsu from? What is his lineage and rank?" It answered "What will I learn?", listed the menu areas and pushed "Pick your 5 tactics". For my exact worry that looks like the classic dodge. Follow-up, "How many hours a day is Rick himself teaching in the two weeks?": an honest "I do not have that one written down". Respect.

## Legit or gimmick

**UNSURE, leaning legit.** I decided the man is real at the VHS tape and the table-roll video, right at the bottom of the page. What keeps me off LEGIT: no named teachers, the "Ninjitsu" spelling (the 80s American-ninja tell, though "most people spell it ninjutsu" is disarming), the PDF's "at least 3 times faster than the average school", and Instructor in one year.

## Moments of friction

1. **Who you learn from / lineage.** Expected: where Rick trained and under whom. Happened: "25 years", press list, "his own system". Felt: the one gap a ninjutsu skeptic checks first. **Blocker** (for booking now).
2. **Chat, lineage question.** Expected: an answer or an honest "not written down". Happened: a confident answer to another question plus a sales button. Felt: dodged. **Would-quit** for a less patient skeptic.
3. **Instructor card.** Expected: a long road to teaching. Happened: "1 year. All five belts, yellow to red, the fast way", with a blank certificate reading "Your name". Felt: belt mill. **Annoyance, close to would-quit.**
4. **Price math.** One belt is $3,997, yet Instructor gives all five plus a certificate for $8,994, and Mastery is $15,988 for the same five, slower. Does not add up. **Annoyance.**
5. **Urgency.** "Only 12 spots left for 2027", "save $2,000", friend free. Felt: salesy next to an "easy-going" pitch. **Annoyance.**
6. **When.** "Rick often rotates ... when he is available." Felt: hedge on the one thing I am paying for. **Blocker** (I write first).
7. **Best proof buried.** The two real videos sit near the bottom, after the prices. **Annoyance.**
8. **Pocket Dojo** free or for sale, and for how long. **Annoyance.**
9. **2 weeks "Pick and book"** threw me back to the top quiz instead of a booking step. **Annoyance.**
10. **Dates.** First start 15 January 2027; my winter break is over by then. **Cosmetic** (summer works).

## Moments that worked

- The VHS tape, the table-roll clip, class photos with real belts.
- "What it is not": no sparring unless you ask, not a boot camp.
- "(most people spell it ninjutsu)": honest, no fake Japanese line.
- The free 20-page PDF before you pay.
- "Gamers and nerds", the game room, "the rest of the day is yours".
- The honest chat miss; the Pocket Dojo screenshots.
- Clear schedule: 8 to 10 morning, optional evening, weekends free.

## Words I did not understand

HI Ninja (HI what?), PSC Self-Defense Program, Sensei Seminar (Rick, or a sensei?), Guide, RTMS (explained later, not at first use), "Mini-Martial Art System via Stances", Tew's Tour.

## Buy, write or leave

**Write.** Question box, topic "The training and the belts": who did Rick train under, how many of the morning seminars does he teach himself, and is July open.

## The one change

In "Who you learn from", add one plain paragraph on where Rick's own ninjutsu came from (teachers, years, ranks, then why he built his own system), and give the chat the same answer instead of "What will I learn?".
