# Anil, the bookkeeper who checks the maths (1 Oct 2026, round seven)

Laptop, 1280 x 800, headless Chromium. Start page https://ricktew.com/aininja/. About 20 minutes in character. The page is 18 screens tall at this size.

Safety: the two mail endpoints (ricktew-contact, ricktew-intake) were blocked before any page load in every run. The blocked count was 0 in all six runs: nothing even tried to reach them. The mailbox was filled (name and a made-up example.test address) and never submitted, and the mailbox run saw no POST of any kind. The chat box and the quiz sent nothing.

## Verdict in one line
I would write to him, once, with two questions the page could not answer. What I would tell my two clients is: "The prices are flat and honest. The quiz will even tell you when the big one is not worth it. But it is only worth $2,222 if the admin it drafts is about 14 hours a week at $60 an hour, or about 28 at $30. And remember, month one is one desk drafting replies." The quiz lost a point with me when it put a dollar figure "by your own numbers" on an hourly value I had refused to give.

## First impression (top screen, before scrolling)

"The Dojo where my AI ninjas work" told me nothing concrete, and there was no price and no list of what it does. "Not a pitch." next to a big "I want this Tew!!" button made me read the rest more carefully, not less. I only knew what was actually on offer and what it cost on screen 2, at the two price cards. Noted as asked; I am not proposing any change to this screen.

## Moments that worked
- Two flat prices on screen 2, no "contact us for pricing": "$222 to build, then $99 a month" and "$2,222 a month, flat ... Any number of people. The AI is in the price."
- The cost FAQ lists what is NOT in the price: "ad spend, software your business already pays for on its own, and building a product you sell". Exclusions stated up front are rare.
- The quiz, run one: "by your own numbers that is about $1,050 a month back against $2,222 a month. It does not add up yet, and I would rather say so than sell it." The most persuasive line on the page for me, because it argued against the sale.
- The quiz, run three (one or two hours a week, under $20): "You run a tight ship ... I am not going to invent some." A quiz that can say no is a quiz I can recommend to a client.
- "Honest math:" printing the formula at all. Most sites hide it.
- "What happens if you disappear? You lose the window, not the room." Data leaves with you, "I keep no copy", "nothing is charged after". Clear exit terms.
- The Terms: "No long-term contract, no cancellation fee." Build rate $222 per 2 hours and $2,222 per 20 hours agree with each other ($111 an hour).
- The chat's misses: "I do not have that one, and I would rather say so than guess." Better than a made-up answer.
- The chat, on R2: "Four businesses run on the stack I manage today ... and two pay this exact bill." A small, checkable, honest number.
- "Your team presses every send" and "propose only" at white belt. The control rule is stated the same way everywhere.
- "The people stay unnamed on purpose." Better than fake logos.

- "Is the 2222 a month before tax or does it include VAT or sales tax?": Honest miss.

- "What do I get in month one for 2222 if only one desk starts?": The cost entry, which does say one ninja at the same flat price. Answered. Repeats the FAQ.
- "I run a bookkeeping firm with 40 clients. Can the money desk do bank reconciliation for each client?": The generic Money Desk description. **A different question:** no yes or no on reconciliation, nothing on 40 clients.
- "How many hours of your own time do I get per month?" (I meant Rick's time): "How many hours a week do I get back?" **A different question.**

The chat added: two clear misses and the R2 detail. Everything else repeated the page.

**Quiz, three runs:**
