# Dana, the gym owner who hates computers (fresh stranger) - 2026-10-01, round six

Played headless on an iPhone 13 profile (390 x 664, touch), live https://ricktew.com/aininja/. The page was 43 phone screens long. About 22 minutes in character: 10 reading, 4 in the chat box, 5 in the quiz, 3 in the mailbox. Both mail endpoints were blocked and logged before the page loaded. No request to either endpoint was made in any run (every script logged "none to mail endpoints"). "Drop it in my mailbox", "Ask Rick this" and "Send me the breakdown" were never pressed; "Talk to me about it" was pressed and only filled the mailbox. Made-up data only: Dana, dana@example.test.

## Verdict in one line
In real life Dana closes the tab at the TewBeDo console (screen 17 of 43) with "$2,222 a month" in her head and no idea whether a two-part-timer studio that only wants the parent emails gone pays that. Read to the end, the quiz's honest "it does not add up yet" is what would make her write, to ask what just the inbox costs.

## Moments that worked
- **"Is this you?"** Four short ticked lines, all me: "You would rather be with a customer than at a screen." "You do not want it explained. You want it done." I nodded at every one.
- **Prices on screens 3 and 4, no hunting.** "$222 to build, then $99 a month" and "$2,222 a month, flat". "Nothing for you to log into." "Cancel anytime."
- **"Your team checks it and presses send"** plus the bottom question "Will an AI talk to my customers without me knowing? Never." The thing I feared most (a robot answering my parents) is answered twice, plainly.
- **"How it works"**: four lines: you tell me the job, the ninjas do the legwork, a person checks it, I manage it and report in writing. The clearest statement of who does what on the whole page.
- **"A gym full of kids, and no time left."** A real kids' martial arts gym, bookings, belts, birthday parties, 850 kids a month, real photos. This is why my friend sent the link, and it made me trust him.
- **"Friends asked about AI, then asked me to do it for them."** "Now his support desk drafts a reply to every customer mail and a person presses send." That is the sentence my friend meant.
- **"The Front Desk: Support replies drafted on auto. You press send."** One line, my whole problem.
- **"One ninja, one job ... usually the inbox. A person presses every send. That rule never bends."** And "Ninjas earn belts the way my gym kids do". As a dojo owner, the belt idea clicked instantly.
- **"You never wanted a CRM. You wanted the phone to ring, the mail answered and the customer remembered."** Best line in the Claudeforce part.
- **Quiz answers that know I exist:** "My phone. Calls, WhatsApp, a diary" and "Paper, honestly". The quiz felt like it was for me, not for tech people.
- **The quiz result's honesty:** "by your own numbers that is about $500 a month back against $2,222 a month. It does not add up yet, and I would rather say so than sell it." Nothing else on the page made me trust him more. Keep it.
- **Chat answer "Can I use it to answer my emails?"** Plain, concrete, and the Tew Tip "Collect the ten replies you send most. That is the training set, and it is already written." is something I could actually do tonight.
- **Chat misses that admit it:** "I do not have that one, and I would rather say so than guess."
- **The Front Desk's "I want this Tew" prefill:** "I want this Tew: The Front Desk. Support replies drafted on auto. You press send. What I do: / What eats the hours right now: / When I would like it running:". Short, and I would not be embarrassed to send it.

## The one change
Answer the small owner's question next to the price cards, not 36 screens below them. Right now the line under the cards is "Your team presses every send. Want the whole day run for you? That is a custom price, and we talk first." That answers the BIG customer. The small one ("I only want the inbox done, I am a two-person shop") has to find a folded question on screen 40 or finish a twelve-question quiz to learn that $2,222 probably does not fit, and even then gets no number for "one desk on its own". One plain sentence there about what just the inbox costs a small business would have kept Dana reading past the console, because she would already have her answer.
