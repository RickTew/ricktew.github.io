# Ken, the office owner who worries who reads his mail - 2026-10-01

Round six. Read on desktop, headless Chromium, 1280 x 800 screen with the text at 125% (the page laid out at 1024 x 640), so one "screen" below is one of my screens at that zoom. The whole page was 35 screens. About 25 minutes in character, plus a short look at the Privacy and Terms pages, because that is the first place a man like me goes.

Safety: the two mail endpoints were blocked before any page loaded. No request to them was attempted in any run (the block logged nothing). I typed only into the visible, labelled "Your name" and "Your email" fields, with a made-up name and ken@example.test. "Drop it in my mailbox" was never pressed.

## Verdict in one line
I would write one careful question, not buy. The page told me the price, the cancel terms and "what happens if you disappear" plainly and early, which I respect, but it never says whose AI reads my clients' mail, where that mail sits, or who at his end can see it, and the chat box dodged that question three different ways.

## Top screen (not judged for change)
About 40 seconds to get "a man who builds an AI setup for your business and runs it with you." I could not say what it actually does for me (mail? books?) until the two price cards on screen 3, about two minutes in at my pace. Before scrolling I noted: two exclamation marks on "I want this Tew!!", the words "ninjas", "Dojo" and "Which ninja?", and a caption under the photo, "Rick Tew, AI version". It is honest to label it. It still made me wonder what else on the page is not the real thing.

## Moments that worked
- **"What happens if you disappear?"** (screen 4): "They are yours in writing, they leave with you, and I keep no copy. What ends with me is the window and the hosting, and nothing is charged after." That is my exact question, answered before I had to hunt. "You lose the window, not the room" took me two reads, then it stuck.
- **"How it works"**: "A person checks it. Nothing goes out without a press." and "I manage all of it and report to you in writing." Plain and steady.
- **"Can I cancel?"**: anytime, end of the paid month, nothing charged after. No tricks.
- **"The people stay unnamed on purpose."** A man who keeps his clients unnamed on his own sales page is a man who might keep mine quiet too.
- **The Front Desk**: "An AI reads every support mail, day and night, and drafts the reply from your own written answers. You read, you press send." The one line that says out loud that an AI reads the mail. Good.
- **Colleagues by Email**: "None of them touch money or the outside world."
- **"Is it safe to let an AI read my inbox?"**: "Attachments are never opened." "Nothing leaves without a person's press." Those two lines are worth keeping word for word.
- **Claudeforce band**: "If three of you need a contact list and nothing else, buy their Starter at $25 a seat. I will not beat that, and I will not pretend to." A plain honest line, and it won me back for a minute.
- **The quiz**: no email asked, and the result named the Front Desk for my shared inbox, which is right. "Estimates, clearly labeled as estimates." When I pressed "Talk to me about it", the box showed me every answer it would send, so I could see exactly what leaves my desk.
- **The chat box misses**: "I do not have that one, and I would rather say so than guess." (for where the computers are, and for breach insurance). Honest.
- **The chat box on contracts**: "If you need your own NDA on top, send it with your message." and "If your industry has a specific rule it must meet, say so in the mailbox and I will tell you straight whether the Dojo meets it." Those two lines are why I would write at all.
- **The mailbox**: "This will not open your email app" and "it always says it is an AI." Honest about the machine.
