# Persona report: Aisha, 31, Toronto, solo, wants real self-defense

iPhone 13, then laptop. Nothing sent.

## Verdict in one line

Rick is clearly a real martial artist and the page says "women who want self-defense", but it never tells me who teaches my class, whether other women will be there, or how much a stranger will be grabbing me, so I write first.

## First 10 seconds

"Martial Arts Ninja Course by Rick Tew", Koh Samui, and a bright gym photo of Rick, an Asian woman and a man in guard stance. A woman in the first picture: good. A beginners' course on holiday in Thailand. It wants me to press Book now or pick 5 "tactics". I would press Self-defense, not Book now. "HI Ninja" means nothing to me, and "a private group workshop" confused me: private, or a group?

## Would I book

Ask first, through the question box. I would pick 2 weeks ($2,222): I have three weeks in Thailand and want the third to travel. What stops me:

1. Who teaches me. The headline says "by Rick Tew", but When says "Rick often rotates between the morning and evening sessions when he is available". When he is not, who is?
2. Other women. Nothing on group size or how many women come.
3. Body contact. "No sparring unless you ask" is great, but the self-defense photos are locks, pins and holds from behind. Can I pair with a woman? Can I say stop?
4. "All sales are final" on $2,222 USD (about $3,000 to me) before those are answered.

## The photos

They make it real: many places and years, real students, not stock. Convinced me: the hero photos with women in them. The best one for me, a woman in a salmon top breaking an arm grab, is exactly what happened to me, and I nearly missed it: the self-defense box had moved on before I reached it and it took about 35 seconds to come back. In my 10 seconds I saw a takedown, a lock and two men on a hilltop. On the laptop, when I looked, all six photo boxes showed only men, mostly in black. Several look old. The man in a neon shirt holding a knife, and Rick leaping between rock towers at the Grand Canyon, read as risky, not reassuring. There the rotation annoyed me.

## The bonuses

WinJitsu: Rick's training for the mind. Coaching with him on focus, goals and self-doubt, his books, and a free month of an app. "Beat the self-doubt" spoke to me, because I froze. But the books "join your belts from orange on", so I think I do not get them in 2 weeks. Pocket Dojo: an app with the belt lessons to keep practicing at home, where you can ask Rick a question. Both say "with every stay", so they read as bonuses, until the Pocket Dojo box ends on a big red "See the Home Study Program" button, which looked like something to buy. The coaching made the price feel slightly more worth it; overall the two long boxes sat between me and the schedule.

## Welcome

Yes: "WOMEN WHO WANT SELF-DEFENSE: Self-defense is on the Ninja Menu, and there is a women's dorm." "Not a fight camp. No sparring unless you ask. Not a boot camp." The self-defense menu: "Jam, Block & Run ... then get away", "Phases of Combat ... so you can escape or defuse it", "Combat Reality: what actually works, stripped of the showmanship", "Natural Reactions: train a move until it happens on its own" (my freeze, named). Free airport pick-up. Sibel K.: "He knows exactly how far he can push his students."

No: "Gamers and nerds", "a gamer hostel", race car simulator and Xbox read like a young guys' place. Photo sets dominated by men. "Knife, after a waiver."

## The chat

I asked: "Are there other women in the group, and how much body contact is there with other students?" It answered "How much does it cost?" with the price list. It did not listen. Rephrased, "Will there be other women training?" and "Is there a lot of physical contact with strangers?" both got the honest "I do not have that one written down" and an offer to send it to Kuno (I did not). "Who teaches the classes, is it Rick himself?" gave back "when he is available": the worry, not the answer. Helpful on logistics, not on my real questions.

## Legit or gimmick

Rick is real: decided at the press list (Black Belt magazine), the years of photos and the self-defense menu. Worth $2,222 for me: not yet, because I do not know who will have their hands on me. **Vote: UNSURE** (leaning legit).

## Moments that worked

- The women's self-defense box in Who.
- "What it is not": no fight camp, no sparring unless you ask.
- The self-defense menu's plain descriptions.
- The woman breaking an arm grab; hero photos with women in them.
- The clear day: 8 to 10 am, optional 8 to 10 pm, weekends free.
- Free airport pick-up; the honest "No private rooms ... shared men's or women's dorm".
- The November friend offer: bringing my friend would make me feel safer.
- The chat saying "I would rather say so than guess" instead of inventing.
- The booking form's plan summary.

## Words I did not understand

HI Ninja, PSC (the photo badge), RTMS, Sensei Seminar, "private group workshop", Guide (as a title), Tonfa, Triangulation, Magnificent 7's, "Mini-Martial Art System via Stances", "Strikes and Kicks form", WinJitsu vs Pocket Dojo, Strategic Intervention Coach, Tew's Tour.

## Buy, write or leave

Write: the question box, asking who teaches, how many women, and how partner drills work.

## The one change

Add one FAQ, also taught to the chat: "I am a woman coming alone for self-defense. Who teaches, who else is there, and how much contact is there?" Answer it plainly: who teaches each class, a usual group size and how many women, that you can pair with a woman and say stop, and how the women's dorm is kept safe.
