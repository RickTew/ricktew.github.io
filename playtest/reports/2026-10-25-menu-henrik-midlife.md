# Ninja Menu round, 25 Oct 2026: Henrik, 54, Copenhagen (henrik-midlife)

Laptop 1280 x 800 first, then iPhone 13. About 56 screenshots. Nothing typed, nothing sent.

## Verdict in one line

A serious page with a clever menu, but I finished it unable to say who would actually teach me most mornings, what I would eat, or whether a 54-year-old beginner fits in, so I would write first, not book.

## First 10 seconds

A two-week introduction to martial arts on Koh Samui, by Rick Tew, "not a boot camp". On the laptop the quiz sits beside the headline and the gym photos, so I knew at once what was wanted: pick techniques. I would start it. Pressing the red "Take the quiz" on the laptop only nudged the page 54 pixels. On the phone there is no photo above the quiz at all.

## The quiz

Fine, not a chore. Five picks is right. The one-line explanations are good: plain, specific, no jokes ("Easy to learn, hard to hurt your own hand" sold me the Palm Heel Strike). My week: Horse Stance, Flat Fall Back, Combat Reality, Pivot Shuffle Shift & Step, Palm Heel Strike.

Confusing:
- The belt colour on every technique. May a beginner pick a red belt item? Nothing says.
- "Ninja Tools" means weapons. Say so. "Blinding Powder" raised an eyebrow.
- "Body Movement" lists cartwheels, kip-ups and hand springs. At 54 that worried me.
- "adapted for RTMS": never explained on this page.
- Phone: after each pick, "Pick from another area" is two screens down.

## The moment to book

"Your Ninja Week is ready" was clear: choose a stay, then Book. Stays: 2 weeks $2,222, 3 weeks $3,222, 1 month $3,997, a belt (2 months) $7,994. But a Week 2 row of empty circles and "5 of 10 picked" appeared. Must I pick five more? It let me book anyway, but the "ready" headline felt untrue.

"Add a week for only $1,000" is honest arithmetic, though the button "Add a week, $3,222" under it made me add it up. The month tempted me more: I have six months. "Only" is salesy. "All sales are final" I respect; "dates can move within a year" softens it properly.

## The program as I understood it

- **Sensei Seminar:** a group class, 8 to 10 each weekday morning, built from everyone's picks. Taught by Rick, I assumed from the headline. The FAQ says Rick teaches "at least 2 classes a week" and Guides, former students, teach the rest. So most mornings it is not Rick. That changed my view of the product.
- **Hours:** two a day, four with the evening class.
- **Review class:** optional, 8 to 10 pm, to practise the morning. Yet "A day on Samui" says "Not a morning person? Come to this one instead", which reads as a repeat. (Guessed: both.)
- **WinJitsu:** Rick's "mental martial art", coaching for focus and goals, included. How much, one-to-one or group? Not stated. (Guessed: informal.)
- **Housing:** a shared six-bed bunk dorm at NerdHostel, a gamer hostel across the street, no added cost. A private room I book myself; the price does not drop.
- **Food:** "simple meals if you need a little boost". Included? How many? Dorm only? (Guessed: some, dorm only.)
- **Belt program:** two months per belt, yellow first, $7,994 a belt, five belts in ten months, Rick's Modern American Ninjitsu. Clear. No word on how a belt is tested.

## Make it pop

**The one change:** at "Your Ninja Week is ready", a short "Your two weeks, plainly" box: who teaches which class, hours a day, how much WinJitsu coaching and in what form, the bed, and exactly which meals. That is where I decide.

More ideas:
1. **First screen:** one current photo of Rick teaching adults, and a line on who usually comes (ages, beginners). The grid shows people in their twenties.
2. **Each technique list:** one line on what the belt colours mean for a two-week guest.
3. **FAQ:** add "Am I too old?" and "How is a belt tested?".

**Cut:** "New to AI? Rick can help with that too" (blurs the offer), the Gaming Zone card, "only" in "only $1,000", the empty Week 2 circles on a two-week stay.

## Legit or gimmick

**UNSURE.** The press list, the Robbins-Madanes coaching certificate, the "What it is not" box and "the old camps were hard core, these two weeks are not that" read as real. Then the FAQ on who teaches pulled me back, and the About page I opened to check him calls the program "Gooffy Ninja ShhT!!, zero seriousness, all in", contradicting my reason for going and this page's "you pay for the training". The 7.5-minute intro video is old footage, uncaptioned, length not shown.
