# Sofia, the salon owner who already has a booking app - 2026-10-01 (round six)

Read on a phone (headless Chromium, iPhone 13 profile, 390 x 664, touch), at https://ricktew.com/aininja/ . The page is about 42.5 phone screens and about 3,800 visible words. About 20 minutes in character.

Safety: the two mail endpoints (ricktew-contact, ricktew-intake) were blocked before every page load and every request was logged. Zero requests to either were attempted in any run: nothing was blocked because nothing tried. I never pressed "Drop it in my mailbox", never pressed "Send me the breakdown", and never typed into any mailbox field. The only typing was in the chat box.

## Verdict in one line
In real life I close the tab at the TewBeDo console, about 16 screens in, before the spa example that was made for someone like me; reading on to the end I would send one short question, because the quiz told me straight that $2,222 a month does not add up for my salon yet, and nobody selling to me has ever said that.

## Moments of friction (the core of the report)

1. **Top strip, "Which ninja? / Take the HI Ninja door"**, first second. I expected the guy from the Instagram post. I got a black bar asking me which ninja I want and offering me a door. I did not know there were doors. Felt like I had landed in the middle of something. **cosmetic**.

2. **Under the photo, "Rick Tew, AI version"**, screen 1. I expected a photo of the man. The caption tells me the photo is made by AI. So is he real? Why would I trust the face if he tells me it is not his? For a page that keeps saying a real person checks everything, this made me squint. **annoyance**.

3. **The number strip, "6,242+ rounds of changes since March"**, screen 2. I do not know what a round of change is. "25+ years a ninja" and "22+ years a nerd" were cute. "100+ solutions built": solutions to what? I scrolled past. **cosmetic**.

4. **The two plan cards, "Basic plan · R2 Hosting" and "Full plan · Your Dojo"**, screens 3 to 4. I expected to find out if this is for a salon. R2 Hosting reads as a website for $222 then $99 a month. I live on Instagram and my bookings are in my app; do I need a website? Then $2,222 a month. That is more than ten times my booking app. That is a stylist's wage. My first thought was "this is for bigger places than mine". "Any number of people" helped a little, I have nine. Neither card says a word about the booking app I already pay for. **annoyance, close to would-quit**.

5. **"I want this Tew"**, everywhere. At first it reads like a typo of "too", and a button that says "I want" before I know what I want feels pushy. The grey line under the cards ("The 'I want this Tew' button writes your pick into my mailbox at the bottom of this page. You add a line and send it.") explained it, and that line is good. Then I pressed it on the R2 card: the page threw me about 37 screens down to a form, with the Name box outlined in red like I had already done something wrong. The message box was filled in for me ("I want this Tew: R2 Hosting, $222 to build, then $99 a month. I build it, I host it, I keep it running." plus "What I do:", "What eats the hours right now:", "When I would like it running:"). That part was clear. But I lost my place on a very long page and had to scroll all the way back. **annoyance**.

6. **"The first questions", "What does it cost?"**, screen 5. I opened it hoping for "what about my booking app". It is the two cards again, in one long paragraph. Buried in it: "Not in the price: ad spend, software your business already pays for on its own". The only way I worked out what happens to my booking app was by reading that sentence backwards: I keep paying for it. That is my biggest question and it is answered by accident. **annoyance**.

7. **"Five words · five boxes"**, screen 6. A drawing of a box on a line, a Replay and a Next button, and "A Dojo is the setup I build for one business." I did not want a vocabulary lesson. Skipped. **cosmetic**.

8. **The story, "Five months. Solutions and goals. One reason."**, screens 7 to 20. The gym part pulled me in: bookings, birthday parties, "5,000+ app bookings since April", and the till that charged "$5 per staff member per month" (I count every one of those, that line was mine). Then it turned into his songs, his books with five methods called ACE, CMT, MAK, MBS, NRG, a board game he drew as a kid, pixel games on the App Store, and then **"TewBeDo: the console nobody sold, so I built it"**: a dark screen with VIEW, BE, DO, AGENTS, INTEL, TRIP and a "security sweep, one button". This is a tech man showing me his toys. This is where I would have closed it and gone back to my cut. **would-quit (screen 16.5)**.

9. **"Just a few of the apps I've made recently"**, screen 21. The first phones in the slider are games: a dungeon, robots and cyborgs, "five in a row". The spa (SabaiSen) is in the list but I had to wait for it. **annoyance**.

10. **"The website is not the machine. It is the handle."**, screens 22 to 25. This was the most "me" part of the whole page: a spa app with "Book a treatment", "Join now", "My membership", and underneath "Every booking, recorded", "Staff, named on every transaction", "Agents drafting the email". But it is 22 screens down, after the games. Most salon owners would never see it. And it still does not say if this engine replaces a booking app like mine or sits on top of it. **annoyance**.

11. **"The solutions. Every one ran on me first."**, screens 26 to 28. Eleven rows. I opened The Booking Desk: "People book you without an account or email ping-pong. Reminders, reschedules and cancels handle themselves." That is literally what my booking app does. So is this instead of it? Not said. Nothing about no-shows. Deposits only appear inside "The helpings", which is closed and labelled "The smaller things people asked for. Tap to open.", so I would never have found "Birthday parties and school groups with a deposit". The Publishing House, The Ad Room, The Video Room, The Notebook, Colleagues by Email: not for me. **annoyance**.

12. **Chat box, my first question**: "will this replace my booking app or do i still pay for that too". It answered a different question, "Is there a real person behind this, or is it all AI?". On the phone the matched question had already scrolled off the top, so I just read an answer about a real person that had nothing to do with my booking app. I nearly did not notice it was the wrong answer. **annoyance**.

13. **Chat box, "can it stop no shows and take deposits"**: it answered "Who owns what gets built, and do I have to manage any of it?". Wrong again. No-shows are half my evenings. **annoyance**.

14. **"Somebody told you to get Claudeforce? Here is the honest bill."**, screens 32 to 35. Nobody told me to get Claudeforce. I have never heard of it. Salesforce, CRM, "Ten people, one year: $38,400 to $66,000", "a Salesforce partner by the day", then a company called Marlow. This is a big-company world. Three screens of somebody else's prices. If I were still here, I would leave here. **would-quit**.

15. **The quiz, question 4 button "That is all of them →"**. I thought it meant "tick all of them". It actually means "I am done ticking". **cosmetic**.

16. **The quiz result**: "Some of what you described is not a desk sitting on a system you already own, it is the system. Building the thing itself is separate work, scoped and agreed in writing before the clock starts." I told it I rent software that does not quite fit. Does this mean he wants to replace my booking app? Build me a new one? For how much? Lost me for a second right after it had won me. **annoyance**.

17. **"The questions that come up every time"**, screen 39. "Can somebody's AI assistant deal with you instead of them?" and "Does anyone test it before my customers see it?" with "Simulated players go first": these are questions nobody in a salon has ever asked. **cosmetic**.

18. **The mailbox, "One message. I read every one."**, screen 40. Two ways in: the form, or "email aininja@ricktew.com and see what comes back". Which one do I use? And the floating "Take the quiz / Ask" bar covers the small print under the send button on my phone. **cosmetic**.

## Moments that worked

- "You do not want it explained. You want it done." That is me, word for word.
- The R2 Hosting card is plain: "Nothing for you to log into." "First month included. Cancel anytime." I could repeat it to another salon owner.
- The line under the cards that explains what "I want this Tew" does. Without it I would not have pressed anything.
- "How it works" in four short steps, especially "A person checks it. Nothing goes out without a press." I do not want a robot talking to my clients in a voice that is not mine.
- The till story: "$5 per staff member per month just so they could ring up a sale under their own name. So I built my own." I felt that one. Every salon pays a per-chair or per-staff fee for something.
- The spa example: "'None of my customers want to use an app.' Fine. The engine was never for them." Half my clients still phone. That landed.
- The chat box on Instagram DMs (it matched me to WhatsApp, LINE or Facebook): "Email first ... Another channel is a build, not a switch. If your customers live on WhatsApp or LINE, tell me which one and what they usually ask." Honest, not a yes to make me happy.
- The chat box saying "I do not have that one, and I would rather say so than guess" when I asked about salons, and its "Ask Rick this" button writing my question plus "What I do:" into the mailbox for me.
- Quiz question 7, "Software I rent, and it does not quite fit what I do": the only moment on the whole page that knew I already pay for a booking app.
- The quiz result's honesty: "by your own numbers that is about $1,050 a month back against $2,222 a month. It does not add up yet, and I would rather say so than sell it." Then two smaller starts. That is what would make me share this with another salon owner: "look, a guy who told me not to buy his big plan".
- "Talk to me about it" after the quiz carried every answer into the mailbox, so I would not have to type my life story with wet hands.
- "Can I cancel?": "Your domain, your content and your data leave with you, and I do not keep a copy." Clear and fair.

## The moment I knew

In **"Hate tech, tired of it, or no time for it. You still want it to work."**, about **4.5 screens down**, at the grey line under the two cards ("The 'I want this Tew' button writes your pick into my mailbox...") followed by "How it works". At that point I could say all four:
- What he sells: a website or small app he builds and keeps running (R2 Hosting), or his AI doing the admin (replies, bookings, payments) with my team pressing send (Your Dojo).
- Who does the computer work: him and his AI. Nothing for me to log into.
- What it costs: $222 then $99 a month, or $2,222 a month flat.
- What to do next: press "I want this Tew" and add a line in his mailbox.

Still missing at that moment: whether this replaces or sits next to the booking app I already pay for; whether it answers Instagram DMs; whether a six-chair salon is meant for the $2,222 plan or the $99 one; what "the audit" actually is (a call, a form, a visit?); and anything about no-shows or deposits.

## Would I write?

In real life on this visit: **no**, I would have left at the TewBeDo console before reaching anything about a spa or the honest quiz. Having read to the end: **yes**, one short message, because the free audit and the quiz's honesty make it cheap to ask.

First line I would type: "Hi Rick, I have a hair salon, six chairs and nine staff, and I already pay for a booking app: would your Booking Desk replace it or sit next to it, and can it answer my Instagram DMs?"

## Did I understand what is sold, what it costs, and what happens next?

- **What is sold: yes.** The two cards: "Your website or small app, with the forms that bring work in. Built, hosted and kept running by me." and "your admin handled as a service. I manage the AI ninjas that draft every reply and file every payment. Your team checks it and presses send." Gap: whether that admin service replaces the booking app I already have.
- **What it costs: yes for his part, no for my total.** "$222 to build, then $99 a month" and "$2,222 a month, flat". What my salon would really pay in total is unclear: the only hint is "Not in the price: ... software your business already pays for on its own", which I read as "keep paying your booking app too". The quiz then told me the $2,222 plan does not add up for me yet.
- **What happens next: mostly yes.** "The 'I want this Tew' button writes your pick into my mailbox at the bottom of this page. You add a line and send it." and the mailbox says "It writes back within a minute ... I read every message and follow up myself, usually within a day." Gap: what "the audit" is in practice (do I need to book a call between clients?).

## The one change

Answer my question on the cards themselves: for a business that already pays for a booking app, does this replace it or sit next to it, and does that change the price? Right now the only answer is hidden in "Not in the price: ... software your business already pays for on its own", and the chat box answers it with a different question. Every salon, spa and clinic owner who arrives already has a booking app, and this is the first thing we all want to know.
