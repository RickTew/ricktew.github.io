# Graham, the guesthouse owner - 2026-10-01 (round eight, quick check)

Laptop, 1280 x 800, headless Chromium. About 18 minutes in character. Read
top to bottom, opened the folds Graham would open, asked the chat box seven
questions, did all twelve quiz questions, pressed "Talk to me about it" and
filled the mailbox as "Graham Test", graham@example.test. Never pressed
"Drop it in my mailbox". The two mail endpoints were blocked before the page
loaded and no attempt to reach them was logged; the mailbox session recorded
zero non-GET requests. Nothing was sent.

## Verdict in one line
I would write, once, with one question, because the quiz told me straight
that $2,222 a month does not add up for my twelve rooms, and that honesty
earned a letter; but the page never told me whether any of this works with
the booking sites my bookings come through, and the smaller start has no
number on it.

## Moments that worked

- The prices are on the second screen, in plain numbers, with the way out
  printed on the card itself: "Monthly, cancel anytime, effective at the end
  of the paid month." I did not have to hunt.
- "Your team checks it and presses send." and "A person checks it. Nothing
  goes out without a press." That is the sentence that let me keep reading.
- "Email first: that is where every desk I have built runs today. WhatsApp,
  LINE, Instagram or texts are a build, not a switch". Honest about what it
  does not do yet.
- "What happens if you disappear? You lose the window, not the room." For a
  man who rents rooms, that landed. "They are yours in writing, they leave
  with you, and I keep no copy."
- "Can I cancel?" is a straight answer with nothing hidden.
- "A gym full of kids, and no time left", with real photos of a real gym
  and real numbers. That is where he became a person to me. "I wanted my
  evenings back" is my 6am in one line.
- The quiz result: "by your own numbers that is about $500 a month back
  against $2,222 a month. It does not add up yet, and I would rather say so
  than sell it." Nobody selling a subscription has ever told me that. This
  is why I would write.
- "Talk to me about it" put my quiz answers into the mailbox for me, with
  two blank lines at the top for my own words. I did not have to retype
  anything.
- Chat box on languages: "A guest who writes at midnight has a draft
  waiting by morning, in their own language." Exactly my mornings.
- Chat box misses: "I do not have that one, and I would rather say so than
  guess." I believe a machine that admits it.
- The Terms (footer link) open with "Plain language on purpose." and they
  are.

## The moment I knew

In "Hate tech, tired of it, or no time for it. You still want it to work.",
at the line "The 'I want this Tew' button writes your pick into my mailbox at
the bottom of this page. You add a line and send it." and the How it works
box right after it: about two and a half screens down, roughly two minutes
in.

At that point I could say all four:
- **What he sells:** AI helpers that draft my guest replies, handle
  bookings and file payments, which my staff check and send; or, smaller, a
  website he builds and keeps running.
- **Who does the computer work:** he does. He builds, hosts and manages it;
  my team presses send.
- **What it costs a place like mine:** $2,222 a month flat for the admin
  help, or $222 then $99 a month for a website.
- **What to do next:** the free audit, by writing in the mailbox at the
  bottom.

Still missing then: whether it works with the booking sites; a number for
the smaller start ("quoted in writing"); what "desk", "Dojo" and "ninja"
mean (the fold just below fixed that); whether I can speak to him; and
whether the man in the photo is real ("AI version").

## Where I would have left

Not out of impatience; I read everything. I would have stopped at the quiz
result, about ten screens down, at "It does not add up yet, and I would
rather say so than sell it." That answered my real question (not at $2,222,
not yet) and I would have pressed "Talk to me about it" from there. Below
that I only saw "Why I do this" and the second set of questions on my way
to the mailbox; I kept reading them to the end for this report.

## What else could go

1. **The fold "More of what I built".** Nine screens of books, games,
   songs, a console and app captures. For a guesthouse it is noise, and the
   one useful idea in it (the waterline: bookings by phone still land in
   the engine) is buried three folds deep.
2. **"Your own Digital Dojo. Built by a sensei."** with its "How it grows"
   fold. Everything in it is said earlier, in plainer words, except belts
   and The Intake, and neither helped me.
3. **The repeats and the rows that are not for a small business.** Cost and
   cancel terms appear on the cards, under the cards, in "What does it
   cost?" and in "Can I cancel?"; the friend story three times; "A call is a
   custom step" twice. Plus "Can somebody's AI assistant deal with you
   instead of them?", "Why the belts?", the HIPAA line, and the
   "rounds of changes" numbers.

## The one change

Answer the booking-site question on the page in plain words. The line I
would change is "It works alongside what you already use, like your booking
app or your till." (in "Does it replace the apps I already pay for?"): say
yes or no about the big booking sites and what the desk does with the mail
they send. Every guesthouse, hotel and holiday let reading this page has
that question first, and right now neither the page nor the chat box
answers it.
