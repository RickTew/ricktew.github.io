# Dave, the owner who hates tech - 2026-10-01 (round six)

Persona: Dave, 58, two-bay auto repair shop, three mechanics, wife does the
books on paper and a spreadsheet. Burned once by a website guy who vanished.
Read on a laptop at the counter: headless Chromium, 1280 x 800, live page
https://ricktew.com/aininja/ (20,164 px tall, about 26 screens). About 20
minutes in character.

Safety: both mail endpoints (`ricktew-contact`, `ricktew-intake`) were routed
to abort before any page loaded, in every script. Zero requests hit the block,
and the mailbox session logged zero non-GET requests. The send button was
never pressed. Made-up name and a `@example.test` address only.

## Verdict in one line
I would not write today, and I would have closed the tab around the console
part of the story; the two price cards near the top told me what he sells and
who does the computer work, but $2,222 a month plus "separate work" with no
number on it, for a shop that runs on the phone, is not something I sign up
for from a web page.

   and I did not pick a door, somebody sent me a link. **Felt:** like I walked

## Moments that worked

- **The checklist under "Is this you?"** "You would rather be with a customer
  than at a screen." "You have tried an app ... It is somewhere on your phone."
  "You do not want it explained. You want it done." That is me, said as facts,
  not as feelings. It did not talk down.
- **"None of it is yours to carry."** Plain. I believed it more than the "one
  button" line before it.
- **The R2 card:** "Built, hosted and kept running by me. Nothing for you to
  log into." That is the whole answer to my one question, who does the
  computer work. And "R2 is me, Rick Tew" explained the odd name right there.
- **"Your team checks it and presses send."** Straight about what is still mine
  to do. I trust that more than "I do everything".
- **"How it works", four steps.** "A person checks it. Nothing goes out without
  a press." "I manage all of it and report to you in writing." Short. Got it
  on one read.
- **The questions under the cards exist at all.** Somebody thought about my
  last website guy. "your customer records. They are yours in writing, they
  leave with you, and I keep no copy ... nothing is charged after." "Can I
  cancel? Anytime."
- **"I am a Ninja Nerd ... That is the deal: I geek out on the machine, you get
  your evenings back."** Plain talk with a bit of attitude. Good.
- **The till story.** "The POS I bought charged $5 per staff member per month
  just so they could ring up a sale under their own name. So I built my own."
  I pay those fees. That one landed.
- **"Another had run a factory on paper since 1998; front office and back, it
  became one system."** Paper. That is my shop.
- **"The website is not the machine. It is the handle."** I am a mechanic, I
  get engines. "Even when every booking still comes by phone, it lands in the
  engine instead of a notebook." Best paragraph on the page for me. "Find out
  where your engine is missing" made me laugh.
- **"If three of you need a contact list and nothing else, buy their Starter at
  $25 a seat. I will not beat that."** A salesman telling me to buy the cheap
  thing from somebody else. Even though the rest of that band was lost on me,
  that line bought trust.
- **The quiz knew I exist.** "A trade. A garage, a plumber, a builder, a
  cleaner." First time on the page I saw my business. "No email needed." The
  result in dollars from my own numbers, and "I will not pretend that is a
  lesser answer" about wanting the hours for family.
- **Chat, "do i have to learn anything":** "No, and that is the point. I do the
  computer work." Sounded like a person. **Chat, the phone question:** "I do
  not have that one, and I would rather say so than guess." I respect that more
  than a made-up yes.
- **"Will an AI talk to my customers without me knowing? Never."** Short, sure.
- **"You run the business. I run the ninjas that do your admin."** The closing
  box line. If the ninja word did not bother me, that is the clearest sentence
  on the page.

## The moment I knew

**Section:** "Hate tech, tired of it, or no time for it. You still want it to
work.", at the line "The 'I want this Tew' button writes your pick into my
mailbox at the bottom of this page. You add a line and send it." About **2.4
screens down**, roughly two minutes in.

At that moment I could say: he builds and runs a website or small app for $222
then $99 a month, or that plus the office paperwork drafted by his AI for
$2,222 a month; he does the computer work, my people press send; and the next
step is a button that writes into his mailbox.

**Still missing at that moment:**
- Which of the two cards is a two-bay garage. No example from a trade until the
  quiz, 20 screens later.
- What "admin handled as a service" means for a shop where customers phone.
  The card says "draft every reply"; reply to what?
- What "the audit" is: a visit, a form, a call? And the word itself scares a
  small business owner.
- Whether anything costs on top. That only came out in the quiz result
  ("separate work"), with no number.
- Who answers when I write. That only came out at the very bottom: an AI
  first, then him.

on top with no price. If my regular vouched for him a second time, I would

> Two bay auto shop, three mechanics, wife does the books on paper. Before I

## Did I understand what is sold, what it costs, and what happens next?

- **What is sold: YES.** From the two cards: "Your website or small app, with
  the forms that bring work in. Built, hosted and kept running by me" and "All
  of that, plus your admin handled as a service. I manage the AI ninjas that
  draft every reply and file every payment. Your team checks it and presses
  send." Not from the top screen.
- **What it costs: YES for the numbers, NO for my shop.** "$222 to build, then
  $99 a month" and "$2,222 a month, flat". Which one is mine, and whether a
  build costs extra, I did not know until the quiz said "separate work, scoped
  and agreed in writing" with no figure.
- **What happens next: HALF.** "The 'I want this Tew' button writes your pick
  into my mailbox ... You add a line and send it" and "The audit first, free".
  What the audit is, and that an AI writes back first, I only found out at the
  bottom ("It writes back within a minute ... I read every message and follow
  up myself, usually within a day").

## The one change

Put a small trade shop into the two price cards: one line that says which card
a shop like mine starts on, what the "admin" is when customers phone instead
of mail, and whether anything costs on top. That answers my four questions in
the first three screens, and it stops the quiz result from springing a build
with no number on me at the bottom.
