The Profile

01 · A life's work

A different kind of black belt.

Born in the USA, hooked on ninjitsu at fourteen, teaching by sixteen and running his own dojo at nineteen. For three decades, Rick Tew has taught one stubborn idea: that anyone can get a Black Belt in what they do.

Rick Tew grew up in the USA, and at eight he ran away to California, chasing a bigger world than the one he was handed. The Boy Scouts gave him goal systems, the outdoors and a taste for leadership. The rest he went and found.

At fourteen he fell in love with ninjitsu. Not the costume, the craft. At fifteen he was on television for his martial arts skills; by sixteen he was teaching; by nineteen he had opened his first dojo, while most people his age were still deciding what to do with theirs.

At twenty-one he moved to Europe to build a system of his own. Over six years based in Holland, and later in Spain, he developed RTMS, Rick Tew's Martial Science: an open, multi-art approach, Modern American Ninjitsu crossed with MMA, built to teach movement, not moves. The system changes daily; the student stays self-powered, never just a copy of the master.

Out of the training came a second art, a mental one. WinJitsu, the True Art of Winning, turns the same discipline on the mind, to beat the negative Ninja of fear, doubt and stress. He wrote it down as the five-book "Be a Black Belt in What You Do" series, one book for each finger of the hand.

Today Rick teaches around the world, from Holland and Spain to California, Thailand, Latin America and the Philippines. He runs NinjaGym in Samui, leads live-in camps and BLAST training tours, and coaches as a Tony Robbins Certified Strategic Intervention Coach. The aim has not changed since the first dojo: hand people the tools, and get out of the way.

The Method

03 · How the work works

Movement, not moves.

Rick's teaching turns on a single idea: train the movement, not the move, and the skill outlives the technique. RTMS keeps the body self-powered; WinJitsu keeps the mind from beating itself. Everything else, the camps, the tours, the books, is in service of that.
